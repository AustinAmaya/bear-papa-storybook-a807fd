Preamble

Morning aboard the RISS began with Papa trying to catch his sock.

“Breakfast is escaping,” Bear said.

“That's my foot's breakfast,” Papa replied.

Outside the window, Earth shone blue. Commander Aero waited beside a folded activity chart. Captain Bolts wore a huge smile on his screen. Beep bumped gently into the chart, then beeped at it.

“Crew game morning,” Aero announced. “A floating parcel relay. Everyone has a part.”

Bear looked from the chart to the little spaceship he was building.

“Can I just build this?”

Papa settled beside him. “Let's hear what they've planned.”

Seeds answered

Chapters

Chapter 1 — The Very Small Door

Bear put the little spaceship into its box. Then he took out his book.

It had a silver submarine on the cover. Inside, an explorer had just found a tiny door at the bottom of the sea.

Bear turned the page.

“Breakfast first,” Papa said, clipping two breakfast pouches to the table.

“I'm reading while I eat.”

“You're reading while you hold your breakfast upside down.”

Bear turned the pouch over. “That too.”

Across the room, Captain Bolts opened the activity chart. His round screen face showed a picture of a parcel passing through three hoops.

“Practice run,” he announced. “From the supply cupboard to the observation dome. No racing. No launching parcels at the captain.”

Beep gave a small beep.

“Especially the captain,” Bolts added.

Commander Aero checked the route with her square-lensed scanner. She had come from the Machine World for the outing, and her wing-pack was secured beside the equipment lockers. Here aboard the RISS, the handrails were all she needed.

“Papa will release the parcel,” she said. “Bear will turn it at the corner. Beep will guide it through the low hoop. Captain Bolts receives it.”

Bear looked up. “Can Papa do my bit?”

“Papa's at the start,” Aero said.

“But this is the bit where they open the door.” He held up the book. “It's a very small door.”

Papa smiled. “Those can be the most interesting ones.”

Bear settled into the reading strap by the window. Earth moved slowly below him, blue and white and enormous. The page was much smaller. Right now, he wanted the page.

The others began laying out the hoops.

Beep brought a green marker to Bear's corner, bumped gently into a storage box, and tried the other side.

Bear watched for a moment, then returned to his book.

From the practice lane came a soft thump.

The empty parcel had reached the corner.

There was nobody there to turn it.

Chapter 2 — Ready Before the Push

The parcel stopped against a padded wall.

Nobody was hurt. Nothing was broken. Captain Bolts' screen displayed a large question mark.

“Interesting route,” he said. “Unfortunately, I am over here.”

Beep beeped from beside the low hoop.

Commander Aero pulled herself gently along a handrail and collected the parcel. “Let's try one section at a time.”

Bear kept a finger between his pages.

He did want to see what happened. He just wanted to see what happened in his book more.

Papa clipped the breakfast things away. “Come look at the corner with me.”

“I'm not joining yet.”

“Come look.”

Bear slipped his bookmark into place and pushed softly from the reading nook. Papa caught the nearby rail, leaving room for Bear beside him.

Aero held the empty cloth parcel. It was soft, with wide handles and a yellow stripe.

“Two rules,” she said. “A tiny push. And wait until your teammate is ready.”

She nudged the parcel toward Papa.

Papa held out his hands. “Ready.”

He caught it without a sound.

“That's easy,” Bear said.

“Your turn to watch the next bit.”

Papa sent the parcel toward the corner. It drifted past Bear's shoulder and continued straight ahead.

Bear caught it before it reached the padding.

“Oh. It doesn't turn by itself.”

“No,” said Aero. “That's the corner person's job.”

Bear turned the parcel carefully and sent it toward Beep. A very small push was plenty. Beep gave two quick beeps, then guided it through the low hoop.

Captain Bolts received it against his round middle.

“Delivery!” His screen grew a little picture of a star.

Bear smiled before remembering his book.

“See? You can do it without me now.”

Aero shook her head gently. “We can change the game. But this version has a place for you.”

Papa didn't hurry to fill the silence.

At the practice lane, Beep moved the hoop closer to the corner. Then Beep backed into the marker and beeped in mild surprise.

Bear helped set it upright.

“Other side,” he said.

Beep turned the other way and settled into position.

Aero opened the real parcel. Inside were soft cloth stars for decorating the observation dome. The practice game had a cheerful purpose: getting the decorations there together.

Bear touched a blue star.

Then he glanced toward the reading nook.

His bookmark was still waiting.

Chapter 3 — A Place at the Corner

Papa rested a hand beside Bear's on the rail.

“I know you want to read,” he said.

“It's really good.”

“I believe you.” Papa waited until Bear looked at him. “And I expect you to take part with the team now. They need you at that corner.”

Bear pressed his lips together.

“Not because your book isn't important,” Papa added. “We're doing something together. I'm taking my turn too.”

“How long?”

“One whole relay, including putting our things away. Then there's quiet time.”

Bear looked at Beep beside the hoop. At Captain Bolts, holding the empty parcel. At Aero, patiently checking the clips.

He let out a slow breath.

“Can I put my book somewhere safe first?”

“Absolutely.”

Bear slipped it into the blue pouch beside the reading nook. He checked the bookmark twice.

“Don't open the little door without me,” he told the cover.

Then he took his place at the corner.

“Ready,” he said.

Aero didn't make a speech about it. She smiled and held up one finger.

“First parcel. Empty. Slow.”

Papa gave it a little push.

Bear caught the handles, turned, and sent it toward the low hoop.

Beep wasn't ready.

The parcel brushed the hoop and turned sideways.

“Oh,” Bear said.

Aero caught it against her sleeve. “Try again.”

“I forgot to ask.”

Beep beeped twice.

Bear looked straight at Beep. “Ready?”

Two beeps. A small forward movement.

This time, Bear waited for both. Then he sent the parcel.

It floated through neatly.

Captain Bolts' screen displayed a green tick.

“Now the stars,” Aero said.

They tucked the soft decorations inside, closed the flap, and began.

Papa released. Bear caught. Beep guided. Bolts received.

The first full parcel arrived at the dome.

Bear laughed. “We did it!”

“Second delivery,” said Bolts. “The long banner.”

This one was folded into a flat bundle, wider than the parcel. They had checked that it fitted through the hoops. But as Bear turned it, one corner began to unfold.

A silver cloth star poked out like a surprised face.

Bear reached to tuck it back.

His sleeve nudged the bundle. It drifted above Beep's hoop.

“Wait!” Bear called.

Everyone waited.

That felt different from having to chase it.

Papa held the spare end of the soft guide ribbon clipped to the bundle. Aero steadied the middle. Beep gave a questioning beep from below.

Bear's cheeks felt hot. He breathed out slowly, just as Papa was doing beside the rail.

“I need to fold that bit again.”

“Take your time,” Aero said.

Bear drew the loose corner in. But the wide bundle still hid Beep from view.

“I can't see the ready signal.”

Captain Bolts tilted his round body to look along the lane. His screen showed a thoughtful line.

“I can see Beep.”

Bear nodded. “Then tell me when Beep's ready.”

Beep moved into place.

“Ready,” Bolts reported.

Bear glanced at Aero. “Are you ready too?”

“Ready.”

“Papa?”

“Ready.”

Bear gave the smallest push yet.

The bundle slipped through the hoop. Beep guided the lower edge. Aero kept the folded middle straight. Papa let the ribbon slide gently through his hands.

Captain Bolts received the banner at the dome entrance.

His screen filled with fireworks.

Very quiet fireworks. Picture ones.

Bear punched one hand into the air, then caught the rail again.

Together, they fastened the banner inside the dome. Blue cloth stars curved above the window, with Earth shining beyond them.

Beep bumped softly against Bear's shoe.

Bear bumped the shoe back, gently.

“Good relay,” he said.

Two happy beeps.

Papa looked across at him. Bear was already reaching for the next decoration.

“Where does this one go, Commander?”

Chapter 4 — Toward Earth

“That star goes beside the window,” Aero said.

Bear clipped it in place. Then he helped Papa stack the empty parcels and return the hoops.

Beep brought the green corner marker. Bear held open the storage pocket, and Beep nudged it inside.

“Last one,” Bear said.

He reached for his book pouch.

Then he looked at his wrist.

“Oh, not again.”

Papa paused beside the rail. “Your watch?”

Bear held up his empty wrist.

“My glow-in-the-dark watch.”

Captain Bolts' screen displayed a small clock, followed by a question mark.

“Missing equipment report?”

“Missing Bear equipment,” Bear said.

Aero secured the final hoop. “When did you last have it?”

Bear looked toward the reading nook, then at the corner, then at the dome.

“I don't know.”

Beep gave a thoughtful beep and moved toward the parcel cupboard.

“I'll look by the window,” Bear said. “Beep can look everywhere else.”

He stopped.

Beep was very small. Everywhere else was very big.

Bear returned to the cupboard.

“Actually, let's do it together.”

Papa handed him the empty cloth parcel. “We can check things before we put them away.”

Bear held it open. Beep looked inside.

One beep. No watch.

They checked the folded banner wrapper next. Then the corner padding. Nothing under it except a small piece of blue thread.

Beep moved left.

“The reading nook is right,” Bear said.

Beep paused. Turned. Bumped gently into the padding.

“Sorry. My right. Your left.”

Papa hid a smile behind his hand.

“We need a better system,” Bear said. He pointed at the window. “Toward Earth.”

Beep gave two bright beeps.

That direction worked for everyone.

At the nook, Bear held the pouch steady while Beep inspected the outside. There was a faint green patch behind its little mesh pocket.

“There!” Bear said.

He opened the pocket and found his watch tucked behind the bookmark ribbon.

For a moment, he stared.

Then he remembered.

“I took it off so the strap wouldn't catch the book pages. I put it with the book to keep it safe.”

Papa raised his eyebrows. “Very safe.”

“Too safe.”

Captain Bolts' screen grew a little picture of a watch inside a book.

“Unusual clock cupboard,” he observed.

Bear laughed so hard he had to hold the rail with both hands.

He fastened the strap snugly around his wrist, then checked Beep's little search corner for anything left behind.

“Your turn,” he said, holding the empty pouch for Beep.

Beep gave one satisfied beep.

All clear.

Together they closed the cupboard, checked its latch, and carried the last folded parcel back to the dome.

No shafts. No secret signals. Just a watch, a book pouch, and a very good place that Bear had forgotten.

Chapter 5 — The Patient Door

Quiet time arrived exactly as Papa had promised.

The hoops were put away. The parcels were clipped into their cupboard. In the observation dome, the cloth stars hung still above the window.

Bear settled beside Papa in a wide resting strap, with a blanket across their knees and his book open between them.

He found his bookmark.

“The tiny door,” he said happily. “Still closed.”

“Very patient door,” Papa said.

Commander Aero secured her scanner and joined them by the window. Captain Bolts dimmed his screen to a gentle smile. Beep settled against a soft padded stop.

Bear looked around.

“Do you want to hear this bit?”

Beep gave two quiet beeps.

“Excellent,” said Bolts. “I enjoy a door with proper suspense.”

Bear read. Slowly at first, then more easily. Papa helped with one long word.

Behind the tiny door, the submarine explorer found a room full of little model boats.

“Not a monster,” Bear said.

“Much easier to tidy,” Aero replied.

Bear laughed, and read another page.

After a while, he lowered the book.

“I liked the banner bit.”

Papa rested his hand over Bear's. “I'm glad you joined us.”

“I still like my book.”

“Me too.”

Bear glanced up at the cloth stars. One blue star was the one he'd clipped beside the window. Beyond it, Earth curved through the darkness.

He closed the book when he was ready and tucked it into its pouch.

This time he left his watch on.

Beep made one soft, sleepy-sounding beep. Captain Bolts' smile became smaller. Aero's scanner was quiet.

Papa drew the blanket a little higher.

Bear leaned close. The day's turning and catching and laughing settled into a warm stillness.

All around them, the RISS hummed gently.

Everyone had a place.

Bear rested in his.
