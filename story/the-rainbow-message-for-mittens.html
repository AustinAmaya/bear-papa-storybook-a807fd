The Rainbow Message for Mittens

Aurora helps Mittens untangle a scrambled rainbow message from RISS and send a gentle thank-you home.

Preamble

After supper, Bear and Aurora, the bright pink kitty, found Papa kneeling beside the three little statues.

Little Polar Bear and Little Red Bear sat together on the patio, peacefully retired. Near the walkway, Little Rainbow Bear watched the neighborhood. He had not spoken. He had not moved.

But a thin silver card now rested beneath his painted paws.

A rainbow stripe crossed the card. In its center was a tiny picture of Mittens, the robotic kitty, beside the RISS symbol and one blinking question mark.

Papa studied it. “Little Rainbow Bear has learned that Mittens needs help.”

Bear looked toward the stars. “Then we’d better understand the message.”

What clue should the silver card reveal, and what should they bring to RISS?

A. The Garden Airflow Pattern — three bent-leaf symbols point toward the hydroponic garden. They bring Papa’s little prism compass.

B. The Tail-Light Trouble — the card shows Mittens’s tail colors flashing in the wrong order. They bring Aurora’s Machine World color-guide badge.

C. The Charging-Nest Mystery — the card shows Mittens’s silver charging nest losing power. They bring a nonmagnetic tester and soft cable clips.

Chapter 1 — The Colors That Wouldn’t Sit Still

Bear tapped choice B on the silver card. “We’ll bring Aurora’s color-guide badge.”

Aurora clipped the small moon-shaped badge to her shirt. Its glass center shimmered through blue, green, and gold.

A shuttle arrived with three soft clicks. Little Rainbow Bear remained perfectly still beside the walkway, but one bright stripe slid across his silver card and pointed toward the stars.

Bear smiled at him. “Message received.”

At RISS, Captain Bolts met them in the airlock. Wrenchley stood beside him with a tool pouch, and Beep rolled in a worried circle.

“Beep-beep-beep!”

“Mittens is safe,” Captain Bolts said quickly. “But her tail projector will not hold one message.”

Mittens padded around the corner on silent magnetic paws. Her silver tail lifted.

Green. Purple. Gold. Red.

Then it flashed a picture of a leaf, half a charging nest, and a crooked rainbow—all stacked together.

Mittens made a puzzled electronic trill.

Aurora held up her color-guide badge. The badge did not change Mittens’s tail. Instead, rings appeared around each color, showing where it belonged.

“The colors are real,” Aurora said. “They’re simply arriving together.”

Papa knelt beside Bear. “Like three people trying to show three different pictures through one window.”

Bear watched another scrambled flash. Leaf. Nest. Rainbow.

“So Mittens isn’t making a bad message,” he said. “She’s receiving too many messages at once.”

Wrenchley opened his station map. “All picture signals pass through the light-message relay near the observation dome.”

Captain Bolts pointed down the blue-handled corridor. “That relay should send one picture at a time.”

Mittens’s tail flashed the crooked rainbow again.

Bear looked at Aurora, then at the badge. “We’ll test the relay carefully.”

When they reach the relay box, what should their first test be?

A. Badge Comparison — Aurora compares the live relay colors with her color-guide badge.

B. Rhythm Count — Bear counts the spaces between each flash to find where the messages overlap.

C. Last Clear Picture — Mittens projects the last message she remembers receiving correctly.

Chapter 2 — The Hallway of Borrowed Colors

The route to the observation dome should have been simple: blue handles, then two yellow turns.

But the first direction panel flashed blue, yellow, blue-yellow, and then a picture of a strawberry wearing a wrench.

Wrenchley stared at it. “That is not an approved station direction.”

Beep gave one amused beep.

Papa held the rail. “The relay trouble may be leaking into the hallway signs. Let’s use shapes and labels instead of trusting color alone.”

Bear read each printed module name. Aurora checked the arrow shapes. Mittens moved beside them with her magnetic paw pads, stopping whenever the floor became a wall around the station’s curve.

At one junction, every arrow pointed left.

“Even the arrow marked RIGHT?” Bear asked.

“Especially that one,” said Captain Bolts.

Bear noticed tiny numbers printed beneath the signs. “Observation Dome is Module Eight. We don’t need the arrows. We can follow the numbers.”

They counted together.

“Six,” said Aurora.

“Seven,” said Bear.

“Beep!” said Beep at Module Eight.

The observation dome opened above them, full of Earthlight. Blue oceans and white clouds floated beyond the wide curved window. Mittens paused to look, and her tail briefly made one perfect picture of home: Little Rainbow Bear beneath a stripe of color.

Then three other pictures crashed across it.

Behind a silver wall panel, the light-message relay hummed. It had three round channels: garden green, station gold, and neighborhood rainbow. They were supposed to blink one after another.

Instead, all three blinked together.

Papa pointed without touching. “A message can bounce like an echo. If the timing gate is stuck, one signal may still be inside when the next one enters.”

Aurora peered through the panel window. “The prism wheel is turning, but it pauses at the same crooked angle every time.”

Mittens placed one magnetic paw beside the panel and waited.

Bear took one slow breath. The lights were busy. He did not have to be.

“We found where the messages tangle,” he said. “Now we can use the test we choose before we open anything.”

Captain Bolts secured the dome door. Wrenchley laid out only the safe tools. Everyone was ready.

Bear glanced at the tiny rainbow image still waiting in Mittens’s tail. Little Rainbow Bear had somehow noticed the trouble all the way from home.

After Mittens is safe and the relay is repaired, how should they thank Little Rainbow Bear?

A. A Rainbow Projection — Mittens projects a bright thank-you above the neighborhood.

B. A Guardian Badge — the RISS crew makes a tiny silver-and-rainbow badge to place beside him.

C. A Return Card — they send home a silver card marked with Mittens’s pawprint and three neat color stripes.

Chapter 3 — The Crooked Rainbow Channel

Aurora unclipped the color-guide badge and held it beside the relay window.

“Badge comparison,” Bear said. “Choice A.”

Captain Bolts switched the relay into safe-test mode. The three channels slowed from a frantic flicker to a steady pulse.

Garden green crossed the badge first. A clean green ring appeared.

Station gold followed. A clean gold ring appeared.

Then neighborhood rainbow struck the badge.

Two rainbow rings appeared—one bright and one pale—almost on top of each other.

Aurora leaned closer. “There are two rainbow signals.”

Papa knelt beside Bear. “One may be the real message. The other may be an echo arriving too soon.”

Inside the relay, the prism wheel turned. Click. Click. Crooked pause.

Each time it paused, the pale rainbow ring bounced backward through the green and gold channels. The hallway signs outside flashed nonsense again.

Captain Bolts checked the control panel. “The relay’s automatic restart begins in one minute. If it restarts while the channels overlap, every picture sign on RISS will scramble again.”

Bear’s chest tightened. The lights seemed faster. The hum seemed louder.

Papa placed one hand gently against his own chest. “I’m going to slow down before I decide anything.”

He breathed in.

Then out.

Bear matched him. Aurora did too.

The relay kept blinking, but the team stopped feeling as if they had to blink with it.

“What is different about the pale ring?” Papa asked.

Bear looked through Aurora’s badge. The bright rainbow made seven neat bands. The pale one had a tiny silver notch through its blue edge.

“There,” Bear said. “The echo carries a silver mark.”

Wrenchley used a ceramic tool to open the safe outer cover. Nobody touched the spinning wheel. Aurora moved her badge slowly along the window until the silver notch glowed.

It pointed to a clear signal tab no bigger than Bear’s fingernail. The tab was supposed to lie flat beside the prism wheel. Instead, one corner had curled upward.

“The wheel catches it at the crooked angle,” Aurora said.

“And the curled tab reflects the rainbow message twice,” Bear added.

Forty seconds remained.

Wrenchley powered down the wheel. It coasted to a stop.

Mittens waited outside the marked tool area. Her magnetic paws would have tugged at the relay, so she planted them firmly on the floor and watched.

Bear held the color-guide badge where Wrenchley could see its clean rainbow bands. Aurora kept the silver notch centered. Papa watched the power light.

With one gentle press from the ceramic tool, Wrenchley flattened the signal tab.

“Ready for test power,” he said.

Captain Bolts restarted the relay.

Garden green passed through alone: a picture of three watered strawberry plants.

Station gold followed: a picture of Mittens’s charging nest, warm and ready.

Then neighborhood rainbow flowed through in seven perfect bands.

Mittens’s tail lifted. This time it projected one clear picture: Little Rainbow Bear standing exactly where he always stood, perfectly still, with the silver card beneath his painted paws. Little Polar Bear and Little Red Bear sat peacefully together on the patio nearby.

Across the picture appeared three small words:

HELP SIGNAL RECEIVED.

Mittens gave a delighted electronic trill. Beep answered with six happy beeps.

“How did Little Rainbow Bear get the card?” Aurora wondered.

Papa smiled. “That part may remain his mystery.”

Bear smiled at the still rainbow figure in Mittens’s picture. He had not spoken. He had not moved. Yet he had noticed a tangled message crossing the neighborhood sky, and help had come.

When they are finally home and ready to settle down, what should make bedtime extra cozy?

A. Patio Stargazing — they sit quietly near the three little bears and find RISS through Bear’s telescope.

B. A Three-Color Drawing — Bear and Aurora draw the green, gold, and rainbow messages and leave the picture beside Little Rainbow Bear.

C. Ceiling Colors — Aurora uses her badge to cast gentle colors across Bear’s ceiling while Papa tells a quiet story.

Chapter 4 — A Rainbow Across Home

Captain Bolts closed the relay panel and placed a green SAFE tag across its latch.

“Repair complete,” he announced.

“And now,” Bear said, “choice A. Mittens should send Little Rainbow Bear a rainbow thank-you.”

Mittens’s tail rose eagerly and projected seven bright bands across the observation dome wall.

Wrenchley scratched his metal chin. “Lovely indoors. Unfortunately, Little Rainbow Bear is not indoors.”

Beep looked toward Earth. “Beep?”

Papa smiled. “We already have a working neighborhood rainbow channel. Could Mittens send her picture through the relay instead of trying to shine it all the way from space?”

Captain Bolts opened the relay’s message tray—not the tool panel—and loaded Mittens’s picture into the safe outgoing slot. Aurora held her color-guide badge beside the preview screen.

The first test appeared above Papa Bear Home on the screen below.

It was tiny.

It was backward.

And its colors read violet, blue, green, yellow, orange, red—with the last stripe missing entirely.

Mittens’s ears tilted sideways.

“That thank-you looks as tangled as the old message,” Aurora said.

Papa pointed to the curved observation window. “The relay is fixed. This is a different problem. The picture flips once when it passes through the station lens and again when the neighborhood receiver places it in the sky.”

Bear studied the preview. “Two flips should make it normal.”

“They would,” Papa agreed, “except Mittens’s tail projector already flips pictures once for a wall.”

“Three flips,” Bear said. “That makes it backward.”

Aurora turned her badge upside down beside the preview. The seven guide colors lined up perfectly—but in reverse order.

Bear grinned. “Send the picture backward from here. Then it will arrive forward at home.”

Mittens rebuilt the image carefully. Violet first. Red last. She added a small silver kitty shape beneath the colors.

Captain Bolts selected LOW, SOFT, and NEIGHBORHOOD ONLY.

“Message away,” he said.

The shuttle carried Bear, Papa, and Aurora home while the signal traveled through the clean relay channel.

When they stepped onto the patio, Little Polar Bear and Little Red Bear sat peacefully side by side. Little Rainbow Bear remained at his neighborhood post, perfectly still. The silver card still rested beneath his painted paws.

Then a gentle rainbow appeared above Papa Bear Home.

Seven colors curved through the evening air. Beneath them glowed the outline of a silver kitty. The light was soft enough that the first stars still showed through it.

Bear waved toward the sky. “That’s from Mittens.”

Little Rainbow Bear did not answer. He did not move.

But when the rainbow faded, Bear noticed a tiny silver star at the corner of the card. It had not been there before.

Aurora blinked. “Did you see that appear?”

“Nope,” said Bear.

Papa smiled. “Mysterious ways.”

They left the card safely beneath Little Rainbow Bear’s paws and went inside. The adventure was finished. Now only a warm, quiet landing remained.

Chapter 5 — Colors on the Ceiling

Upstairs, Bear changed into his pajamas while Aurora placed her color-guide badge on the bedside table.

“Choice C,” Bear reminded her.

Aurora tapped the badge once. Soft colors climbed the wall and spread across the ceiling—not bright station flashes, but slow bedtime bands.

Green drifted past like a quiet garden leaf.

Gold followed like a warm lamp.

Then seven rainbow colors rested together above Bear’s bed.

Papa sat beside him. Aurora curled up nearby, her bright pink fur turning rosy in the gentle light.

“Story, please,” Bear said.

Papa began softly. “Once there was a message that had very far to travel. It crossed a station, a window, the evening sky, and a neighborhood. It became tangled along the way.”

“But someone noticed,” Bear added.

“Someone very still,” said Aurora.

Papa nodded. “He did not need to run after the message. He did not need to shout. In his own mysterious way, he helped it reach the people who could understand it.”

Bear watched the rainbow bands breathe slowly across the ceiling.

“And then we fixed the relay,” he said.

“You noticed the silver mark,” Papa replied. “Aurora compared the colors. Mittens waited safely. Everyone had one useful part.”

Downstairs and outside, Little Polar Bear and Little Red Bear sat peacefully together on the patio. Little Rainbow Bear watched over the quiet neighborhood. He did not speak. He did not move. Beneath his painted paws, the silver card held its new little star.

Far above, Mittens settled into her warm charging nest. Her tail projector blinked one neat rainbow, then went dark for rest.

Aurora dimmed the badge until only a soft blue dot remained.

Papa pulled Bear’s blanket comfortably beneath his chin.

“Goodnight, message finder,” Bear whispered toward the window.

The blue dot faded.

Papa stayed close. Aurora’s breathing became slow and even. Bear’s eyes closed beneath a ceiling that was ordinary again.

The neighborhood was safe. Mittens was safe. Everyone was home where they belonged.

And bedtime landed softly.
