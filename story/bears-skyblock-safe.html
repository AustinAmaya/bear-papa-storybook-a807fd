Preamble

It was a golden afternoon on the Skyblock One-Chunk Island. The grass was square, the water was blue, and the sky went on forever.

Bear had a plan. He had been carrying a stack of stone blocks and a big idea. "We're building a safe," he announced. "A safe for the most important things in the whole world."

JJ grinned from under his red hood. He had his notebook open already. "A safe is a smart idea. I like smart ideas."

Mikey was already digging a hole. "Dirt is easy! Dirt is my favorite!"

Papa stood at the edge of the island, watching the three of them with a warm smile, the wind ruffling his dark-brown fur.

Chapter 1

Then a low rumble rolled across the sky. Something green and shadowy shuffled near the edge of the island.

Bear's ears went straight up. "Trouble!"

Papa knelt down, right at Bear's eye level. "Let's breathe first. In for four. Out for four." They breathed together, one, two, three, four. Bear's shoulders came down.

"Okay," Bear said. "Now we build."

He grabbed a stack of stone blocks and started stacking. Bear set one block down, then another, then another. Each block clicked into place like a puzzle piece. A wall began to rise around the little safe, one row at a time.

"That's a lot of wall," JJ said, flipping a page in his notebook. "Good thinking. Stone keeps out zombies and creepers both."

"Stone is strong," said Bear. "Stone doesn't wobble."

Mikey jumped down from the dirt pile. "I'll dig a trench! Trenches are great!" He scooped out a long, neat ditch right along the outside of the wall. Dirt flew everywhere.

Papa picked up a block and set it on top. "Good. Now the wall is higher than the rumble."

The shadowy shape crept closer. It bumped the new wall, then stopped. It sniffed the stones. Then it turned and waddled slowly back the way it came.

Bear let out a long breath. "It didn't like the wall."

"Nobody likes a wall that's ready for them," JJ said with a grin.

Papa stood up and brushed the dust off his paws. "Well done, team. You saw the danger and you built something to keep it away. That's how it's done."

Bear looked at the wall. It was crooked in one spot, so he fixed it. Then he looked at his friends. "Next, we make a plan. Everybody needs to know where everything goes."

Above them, the sky glowed gold. A single bird flew past the tree, then curved away over the water, as if it had somewhere better to be.

Chapter 2

Bear wiped his hands on his shorts. The wall was done, and the safe sat snug inside it. But the safe needed a lid, and a lid needed wood.

"We need planks," Bear said. "Lots of them."

JJ pointed across the sky. A tree stood on a second little island, far across a gap of blue air. Its leaves were square and bright green. Beside it sat a shallow pool with a small waterfall trickling into it.

"That tree has wood," JJ said. "I can see the trunk from here. But the gap is wide."

Mikey bounced to the edge and peeked over. "Whoa. That's a long way down. Like, a long, long way."

Papa came and crouched beside them. He tapped the edge of the island with one finger. "Before we jump, let's look at the problem. What do we need to cross?"

"A bridge," said Bear.

"A bridge," Papa agreed. "And bridges work best when they have support on both ends. Here's the trick. If we put a block at each end and lay another across the top, the gap closes."

Bear's eyes lit up. He stacked two blocks at the edge, one on each side. Then he carefully set a long plank across the top.

The plank wobbled. Bear wobbled too, arms out, like a tightrope walker. "Whoa, whoa!"

"Slow down," Papa said gently, and knelt again. "Breathe first, then count."

"One, two, three, four," Bear counted with him. The wobble stopped.

"Good," said Papa. "Now we give the bridge a second support in the middle. That way the plank can't bend so much."

Bear added a block in the middle. The bridge held. He looked at it, then at Papa. "It's not wobbling. It's stiff."

"That's right," said Papa. "Triangles and supports make things stiff. Stiff things hold weight."

JJ wrote that down in his notebook, underlining it twice. "Rule number four. Support in the middle."

Mikey sprinted across the bridge first. "Yes! Easy! Come on!" His shell bumped gently as he hopped to the far side.

Bear went next, slow and careful, one foot in front of the other. He counted his steps. Seven steps. Then eight. Then he was at the tree.

JJ followed, and then Papa, walking steady in the middle, just the way he'd shown them.

At the tree, Bear reached up and tapped the trunk with his hand. "Wood," he said happily. "Good, strong wood."

The little waterfall splashed behind them. The pool sparkled. It was a good spot, and it was the first of many good spots, and Bear was already looking for the next one.

"How many planks do we need for the lid?" Bear asked.

JJ flipped to a fresh page. "Let's count."

Chapter 3

Back on the island, Bear stood beside the safe. He looked at the wall, the trench, the bridge, and the pile of planks. Then he picked up a flat stone and a piece of charcoal.

"Everybody, come here," he said. "I have a plan."

JJ, Mikey, and Papa gathered around. Bear knelt and drew a square right in the dirt. Then he placed stones around the edge like a map.

"This is the safe," he said, tapping the middle. "The planks go here, in a neat stack. JJ, you hold the notebook and check each plank as it comes. Mikey, you bring the dirt for the top of the wall. And Papa, you stand by the bridge and steady anything that wobbles."

JJ squinted at the drawing. "Wait. Why is the stack on this side?"

"Because the wind blows from that side," Bear said. He pointed at a tiny swirl of dust in the air. "See? If the stack is on the open side, the wind pushes the planks off the edge. If it's on the safe side, the wall keeps them."

Papa smiled and nodded slowly. "You looked at the wind before you placed the stack. That is careful thinking, Bear."

Mikey stared at the drawing. "This is the best map ever. Maps are my favorite. Well, after dirt."

"Dirt, maps, and bridges," JJ said with a grin. "That's three favorites."

"Four," said Bear. "The safe is my favorite too."

Everyone got to work. Mikey hauled dirt in neat scoops. JJ checked each plank in his notebook. Papa walked back and forth across the bridge, testing each step. Bear carried plank after plank from the tree, his arms full, his shoulders strong, counting each trip.

"Eleven," Bear said, setting down the last plank in the stack. "Eleven planks. That's enough for the lid!"

Then the wind changed.

It came in one big gust, sudden and loud. The air whooshed across the island. The top plank on the stack wobbled, slid, and tipped toward the open edge.

"Look out!" Mikey shouted.

JJ lunged. Papa was already moving. He stepped in front of the stack and held out both arms, his big paws spread wide, like a wall that could not be knocked over.

"Breathe," Papa said, calm and steady, even with the wind whipping past. "In, two, three. Out, two, three."

Bear breathed with him. The plank stopped sliding. It hung on the edge of Papa's arm, safe and still.

"Got it!" JJ cried, and caught the next plank in both hands.

"I've got this one!" Mikey added. He dove for a loose board, and a wall of dirt tumbled gently in front of it, catching the board in a soft pile. "Dirt works!" he said, laughing.

The gust faded. The air went quiet.

Bear looked at the map in the dust. He looked at the stack, which stood neat and tall inside the wall. Then he looked at his friends, all of them safe.

"My plan worked," he said. "But you all made it work too."

"Teamwork," Papa said softly. "That is the real plan, Bear. Your map showed everyone where to stand, and everyone stood there."

JJ wrote that in his notebook with a big star beside it. "Rule number five. The map makes the team."

Mikey yelled, "Rule number six! Dirt is the best!"

"Rule number six," Bear agreed. "Dirt is great."

Bear picked up the first plank and carried it to the middle of the safe. Then he set the next one down beside it. One by one, the planks went down in a perfect row, as neat as the drawing in the dirt.

Papa knelt beside him. "Ready for the lid?"

Bear grinned up at him, his blue eyes shining. "Ready."

Chapter 4

The lid went on smoothly, almost. Bear lifted the big flat top onto the safe, and it slid into place with a satisfying click. Then he tried to close the little side hatch.

It stuck.

Bear pushed. The hatch did not move. He pushed harder. Still nothing. "Hmm," he said, tapping his chin. "Something is in the way."

JJ leaned over. "I see it. There's a stone block jammed in the crack between the wall and the safe. It must have rolled in during the gust."

"Can we pull it out with our hands?" asked Mikey.

Papa crouched and gently tried the block. He shook his head. "It's wedged tight. Pulling might crack the wall."

Bear frowned. Then his face lit up. He ran to JJ's pack and pulled out a shiny blue pickaxe with a diamond head. It sparkled in the sunlight.

"JJ," Bear said. "Can I borrow the diamond pickaxe?"

JJ grinned. "Of course. But be careful. Diamond is strong, but it still needs a gentle touch."

Bear held the pickaxe with both hands. He looked at the stuck block. Then he looked at Papa. Papa knelt down, right at Bear's eye level.

"Breathe first," Papa said. "In for four. Out for four."

They breathed together. One, two, three, four. Bear's grip loosened. His shoulders came down.

"Now look," said Papa. "Where is the block touching the wall?"

Bear leaned in close. "It's on the bottom edge. If I tap the bottom, the block might slide out the top."

"Clever," Papa said. "Tap, don't swing hard. Gentle taps move things best."

Bear lifted the pickaxe. Then he tapped the edge of the stone. Tink. Tink. Tink.

Nothing happened.

"Keep going," said JJ, his notebook open on his knee. "Count your taps."

"One," Bear said. Tink. "Two." Tink. "Three." Tink.

On the fourth tap, a little crack appeared in the dirt beneath the block. Then the stone wobbled. Bear tapped again, very softly. The block slid up and out, like a drawer sliding open, and it tumbled into his waiting arms.

"I got it!" Bear shouted. He held the block up high, grinning.

Mikey cheered. "Bear, you're a pickaxe pro!"

"Thank you, Mikey," Bear said proudly. "Gentle taps, not big swings."

Papa stood and patted his shoulder. "You stayed calm. You thought about where the block was touching. Then you used the tool the right way. That is how a problem gets solved."

Bear carefully set the stone block on the grass. Then he tried the side hatch again. This time it swung shut with a soft click.

JJ looked at the safe, then at his notebook. He drew a little picture of a pickaxe, tapping a block. "Rule number seven," he said. "Gentle taps, not big swings."

"Rule number seven," Bear repeated. "Good rule."

Bear handed the diamond pickaxe back to JJ, handle first, the way Papa had taught him to pass a tool.

"Thanks, Bear," JJ said. "You did great."

Bear looked up at the safe, which now had a lid, a hatch, and a wall around it. He was proud of it. He was proud of himself, and he was proud of his friends.

"Now it's finished," he said.

Papa smiled. "Almost. Let's head home and rest."

Chapter 5

The sun sank low over the Skyblock sky. The water turned gold, then soft pink. The square clouds drifted slowly by.

Papa Bear Home was waiting on the other side of the portal, warm and quiet. The deck creaked under their feet as they came through.

Bear yawned, a big, slow yawn. "My safe is done."

"It is," said Papa. He sat on the top step and pulled Bear close. "And you know what made it work?"

Bear thought about it. "The wall. And the map. And the bridge, with the support in the middle."

"All of those," said Papa. "And something else too. You stayed calm when the wind came. You breathed. You counted. You asked your friends to help. Those things make a team strong."

JJ, sitting nearby with his notebook, nodded. "Rule number eight. Breathe first, then build."

Mikey was already half asleep against a fence post. "Dirt is the best," he murmured.

Bear laughed softly. "Dirt is the best."

Papa tucked a blanket around Bear's shoulders. The air smelled of cut wood and cool evening. Somewhere down the lane, a porch light clicked on.

"Tomorrow," Bear said, his eyes getting heavy, "we can look at the safe again."

"We can," said Papa. "Tonight, we rest."

Bear snuggled close, one hand on Papa's soft fur. The day was done. The safe was finished. And all his friends were safe too.

Goodnight, safe, and goodnight, friends.

Bear took one slow breath, the way Papa had taught him. In for four. Out for four. The porch swing creaked gently, back and forth, back and forth, like a lullaby that had been waiting all day for him.
