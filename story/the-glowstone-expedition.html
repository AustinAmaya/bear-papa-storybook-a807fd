The Glowstone Expedition

Bear and Papa cross into the Nether to trade for glowstone and help Sprout the Golden Creeper.

Premise (Austin, locked)

- Bear and Papa help Sprout (the Golden Creeper, wears gold armor gifted by Bear & Papa). - Trading expedition into the Nether to trade for and bring back something only found there. - Chosen item: glowstone — needed to recharge the fading Green Night-Light Pebble (established lore item). - Tone: fun, with real seriousness — this is a prepared expedition, not a casual trip. - Learning thread: preparedness / trading mechanics (falls under Scientific/Technological Learning — notice a problem, learn the rule of the world, apply it). - New character introduced: a Piglin Trader in the Nether. Canon Minecraft mechanic used: piglins trade gold ingots for goods, and don't attack anyone wearing gold armor — ties directly into Sprout's established gold armor.

Preamble (delivered)

Bear and Papa were stacking cobblestone at the Base of Bravery when Sprout the Golden Creeper came hopping up the path, his gold armor clinking. He held out the little Green Night-Light Pebble — but it was barely glowing at all, just a tired flicker.

"It needs glowstone," Papa said, turning it over in his hand. "And glowstone only grows in one place in this whole blocky world: the Nether."

Bear's eyes went wide. The Nether meant lava, blazes, and piglins who loved to trade — if you brought the right things and knew the right manners.

"This is a real expedition," Papa said. "We'll need to prepare, and we'll need to trade smart."

Chapter 1 (delivered)

Back at the Base of Bravery, Bear was already digging through the chest of supplies. "Armor," he said. "We need armor, Papa. Real Nether armor."

Papa knelt down next to him. "Good instinct. It's hot down there, and things move fast." He handed Bear a chestplate that clinked satisfyingly as Bear pulled it on.

Sprout the Golden Creeper hopped closer, holding the little Green Night-Light Pebble in his front paws. It gave one weak flicker and went dim again. Sprout made a soft worried hum.

"We'll fix it," Bear said. "We just need glowstone."

Papa laid out gold ingots on the workbench, counting them into a neat stack. "Piglins live in the Nether," he explained. "They love to trade — but only if we bring good things and ask nicely. Gold is their favorite."

"I heard about a glowstone factory," Bear said, bouncing on his toes. "Piglins run it. They mine the glowstone and turn it into blocks. I want to see how it works!"

"Then that's exactly where we're headed," said Papa, smiling.

They packed carefully: gold ingots for trading, a bucket of water each in case of lava, and — Papa's idea — flint and steel, tucked safely in Bear's satchel. "Just in case," Papa said. "A good expedition always has a backup plan."

Bear checked his own straps twice, then a third time. He wasn't scared. He was ready. That felt different, and good.

Sprout pressed the dim pebble into Papa's hand for safekeeping and gave two happy little hops — his way of saying *let's go.*

They walked to the obsidian frame at the edge of camp. Papa struck flint and steel against the empty doorway, and violet light rippled to life inside it, swirling like water standing on its side.

"Stay close, listen for my voice, and remember your training," Papa said, resting a hand on Bear's shoulder.

"Stay close, listen, remember training," Bear repeated back.

Sprout hopped through first, gold armor glinting purple in the portal light. Bear took a breath, held Papa's hand, and stepped forward together into the swirling glow.

Chapter 2 (delivered)

The heat hit first — a thick, dry wave that pressed against Bear's face the moment he stepped through. Everywhere, red netherrack stretched like cracked earth, and rivers of orange lava glowed below them, throwing flickering light onto black stone cliffs.

"Whoa," Bear breathed, staying close to Papa's side just like he'd promised.

Sprout hopped ahead eagerly, his gold armor catching the lava-light like tiny fires. He led them along a narrow ridge toward a dark, looming shape in the distance — a fortress built of blackstone, its towers jagged against the orange sky.

A shape rose from behind a pillar — tall, glowing, with spinning fire rods circling its body. Bear froze for half a second, then noticed the teal ribbon tied around one rod.

"Friendly Blaze!" Bear called out, relaxing.

The Blaze drifted closer, rods slowing to a gentle spin — his way of waving hello. He'd helped them before, and he seemed just as happy to see Bear and Papa again. He led them around the fortress to a wide open platform where several piglins stood near stacks of glowing yellow blocks, sorting them with careful snorts and grunts.

"That's it," Bear whispered. "The glowstone factory."

"Watch how they work first," Papa said quietly. "Notice before you ask."

Bear watched closely. Piglins mined chunks of glowstone from a cluster overhead, then carried them to a stone table where they broke the glowing clusters into smooth glowstone blocks, stacking them neatly for trade. It was careful, patterned work — almost like a little factory line, each piglin doing one job and passing it along.

"They like order," Bear noticed. "And gold. Papa, I bet if we offer politely, they'll trade."

"That's exactly the rule," Papa said, pleased. "Piglins trade fair for fair. No grabbing, no rushing. Just an offer, held out calm and open, and they'll usually offer something good back."

They crossed a narrow obsidian bridge over a thin river of lava to reach the platform. Bear's boots wobbled once on the uneven stone, and Papa's hand steadied his shoulder without a word. Sprout hopped along easily behind them, gold boots barely making a sound against the black rock.

At the edge of the platform, a piglin trader looked up, tusks glinting, golden earrings jingling with each little snort. He eyed the gold ingots in Papa's open palm with clear, twitchy interest, tilting his head side to side.

"Ready?" Papa asked, crouching down to Bear's level.

Bear nodded, stepped forward, and held out his own small offering of gold — calm, steady, exactly like he'd practiced back home at the Base of Bravery.

Chapter 3 (delivered)

The piglin trader sniffed the gold, grunted with satisfaction, and set down two glowing piles of glowstone dust in exchange. Papa nodded his thanks. Bear grinned — their first trade, and it had worked exactly like Papa said it would.

"We need whole blocks, though," Papa said, glancing at the stacked glowstone behind the trader. "Enough to fully recharge the pebble."

The trader turned, snorted at a second pile of gold Papa offered, and reached for a full glowstone block, tusks bobbing as he weighed it in his hands.

That was the moment the sky changed.

A low, groaning wail rolled across the platform, echoing off the blackstone towers. Everyone froze. High above the lava, a huge white shape drifted into view, trailing thin curls of smoke, glowing purple eyes narrowing down at them.

"Ghast," Papa said, his voice steady but quick. "Bear — listen to me. We move, we don't panic, and we watch its mouth. It opens wide right before it fires."

The Ghast's mouth stretched open, and a crackling fireball shot down toward the platform, hissing as it fell.

"Left!" Papa called, and they both dashed sideways as the fireball exploded where they'd been standing, scattering sparks and rattling loose stone across the ground.

Sprout hopped in a tight zigzag, gold armor flashing in the firelight, dodging a second fireball that hissed past his ear close enough to ruffle the air. The piglin traders scattered too, snatching up their glowstone stacks and ducking behind blackstone pillars for cover.

Bear's heart pounded, but he remembered his training. *Calm. Move quickly. Watch the mouth.* He kept his eyes fixed on the Ghast, waiting for the telltale wide-open glow that meant another shot was coming.

"There!" Bear shouted, and they both broke right just as a third fireball streaked down, splashing lava dangerously close to the obsidian bridge and sending up a hiss of steam.

Friendly Blaze rose up between them and the Ghast, rods spinning fast, and knocked a fourth fireball clean off course with a puff of fire — it sailed harmlessly into the lava lake instead of toward the bridge.

"Thank you!" Bear called up to him, and Friendly Blaze's rods spun once in reply, quick and glad.

Papa grabbed the last glowstone block from the trembling trader's hands, pressed one final gold ingot into his palm as thanks, and scooped Bear's hand into his own. "Bridge — now — together!"

They ran, boots pounding on obsidian, lava glowing orange just beneath the narrow crossing. Sprout bounded ahead, checking each step before Bear's feet landed, hopping side to side to guide him around a crumbled edge. Behind them, the Ghast wailed again, closer this time, but Friendly Blaze stayed between them and the sky, drifting backward to block its aim, rods glowing brighter with the effort.

Bear could feel the heat of the lava rising through his boots, but he kept moving, one steady step after another, just like they'd practiced back home. He didn't look down. He looked at Papa's hand, and at the far ledge waiting for them.

They reached solid ground on the far side, breathing hard, glowstone blocks clutched safe in Papa's satchel and glowing faintly gold through the fabric.

"We did it," Bear panted, disbelief and pride tangled together in his voice. "We stayed calm. We dodged everything. We got the glowstone!"

Papa knelt down, pulling Bear into a quick, proud hug, both of them still catching their breath. "You watched, you listened, and you moved exactly right. That's not luck, Bear. That's preparation."

Behind them, the Ghast drifted off across the lava sea, losing interest now that its targets were gone. Friendly Blaze gave one last friendly spin of his rods before turning back toward the fortress, rods slowing to their usual gentle rotation.

Sprout hopped in a happy circle, gold armor glinting, holding tight to the satchel full of glowing treasure, humming a small, pleased tune all his own.

Chapter 4 (delivered)

They made their way back toward the portal, Sprout hopping proudly with the satchel of glowing treasure, Bear practically bouncing with the good kind of tired that comes after a job well done. But when the swirling violet doorway came into view, something was wrong.

The portal frame stood dark and silent. No shimmer, no glow — just cold, empty obsidian, blackened along one edge where a stray fireball must have scorched it during the chase.

Bear's stomach dropped. "It's out."

Papa knelt by the frame, running a hand along the blackened stone, checking it carefully. "The fire knocked it out. We'll need to relight it before we can cross back."

For a second, worry flickered across Bear's face. Then he remembered something, and his hand went straight to his satchel strap.

"Flint and steel," he said, pulling it free. "You said a good expedition always has a backup plan."

"That's exactly right," Papa said, stepping back to give him room. "Go ahead, Bear. You've got this."

Bear held the flint and steel Papa had packed that morning — it felt like a lifetime ago now, back at the Base of Bravery. His hands weren't shaking. He struck the flint against the steel, once, twice, and on the third try, a spark caught the obsidian frame.

Violet light rippled to life, swirling exactly the way it had at home.

"You did it!" Bear said to himself, grinning wide, and Sprout hopped in a delighted circle, gold armor clinking happily against the stone.

But as they stepped closer to the portal, a low rumble sounded from somewhere behind the fortress — another Ghast, or maybe the same one, drifting back their way, drawn by the fresh light.

"No rushing panic, though," Papa said calmly, echoing their earlier rule. "Just a steady, quick walk. We have time. We planned for this too."

Bear took a breath, held Sprout's paw on one side and Papa's hand on the other, and the three of them walked — quick, calm, together — straight through the shimmering doorway.

The heat vanished instantly, replaced by the cool evening air of the Base of Bravery. Behind them, the portal's glow dimmed to a soft, steady hum, safely back on their side of the world, the Ghast's rumble fading with it.

Bear let out a long breath he hadn't realized he was holding, and laughed. "We made it. And I fixed the portal all by myself!"

"You did," Papa said, ruffling Bear's hair. "That's not something everyone could do calmly under pressure. I'm proud of you."

Sprout set the satchel down carefully in the grass and pulled out the glowing glowstone blocks one by one, turning them over in his paws with a soft, happy hum, gold armor catching the last of the evening light.

They had everything they needed. Now it was just a short, easy walk home.

Chapter 5 (delivered)

Back at Papa Bear Home, the evening had cooled into that soft blue hour before bedtime. Sprout sat on the deck, the little Green Night-Light Pebble cradled in his paws while Papa carefully fit a sliver of glowstone into its center.

The pebble caught the light and glowed — warm, golden, and steady, brighter than it had been in days.

Sprout let out a happy hum and hopped in place, hugging the glowing pebble to his chest like a favorite toy.

"Better?" Bear asked, grinning.

Sprout nodded and pressed the pebble briefly against Bear's hand, warm as a little sun. It was his way of saying thank you, and Bear understood it perfectly.

Papa settled onto the top step, patting the spot beside him. Bear climbed up and leaned against his side, finally feeling how tired his legs were after all that running and dodging.

"That was a real expedition," Papa said. "Lava, a Ghast, a broken portal — and you handled every bit of it."

"I was a little scared when the portal went dark," Bear admitted. "But then I remembered we packed the flint and steel. So I wasn't stuck."

"That's exactly the point of preparing," Papa said gently. "It's not about knowing everything will go perfectly. It's about being ready when something doesn't."

Bear thought about that, watching Sprout doze happily beside the softly glowing pebble. "So next time something scary happens, I just check what we packed?"

"Exactly," Papa said, smiling. "Calm first, then plan, then act. You did all three today."

Little Red Bear and Little Polar Bear sat quiet and warm on the patio nearby, watching the pebble's glow, peaceful as ever.

Sprout gave one last contented hum, curled up with the pebble tucked safely against him, and closed his eyes.

Bear yawned, the good, heavy kind of yawn that comes after a big day. Papa wrapped an arm around him.

"Home safe," Bear murmured.

"Home safe," Papa agreed. "Every time."

Above them, the first stars blinked on, and the little pebble glowed steadily beside them — proof that even the trickiest trip ends in something warm.
