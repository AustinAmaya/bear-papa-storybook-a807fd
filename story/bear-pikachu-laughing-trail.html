Preamble

Papa was doing his very best impression of a sandwich.

It was not going well.

“Sandwiches don't walk like that,” Bear said.

“This one has somewhere to be.”

Papa shuffled sideways along the sunny trail, holding his hat flat on his head like a slice of bread. Bear laughed so hard he had to stop walking. Then Papa announced that he was a sandwich who had forgotten his cheese.

That made it worse.

Bear took one giggly step into the grass.

Click.

Under his shoe was a small block with a Poké Ball symbol on top.

The lid popped open.

“Pika!”

Up sprang a little yellow Pokémon with black-tipped ears, red cheeks, and a lightning-bolt tail.

“Pikachu!” Bear cried.

His hand went straight to his pocket. His fingers closed around his Master Ball.

Papa lowered his sandwich hat.

“Well,” he said. “That is definitely not cheese.”

Pikachu tilted his head. Bear grinned. The sunny trail suddenly seemed full of possibilities—and their laughing adventure had only just begun.

Seeds answered

1. Bear sets up electric lightning-bolt target practice for Pikachu. 2. An already-fallen tree blocks the path. Pikachu helps clear it with sparks and Bear has water to put them out. Adaptation: Pokémon make-believe; Papa leads the work and handles water, no real-world fire instructions. 3. Bear mistakes Pikachu for Papa, then apologizes to Pikachu.

Papa leads safe make-believe work; Pikachu uses magical sparks and water settles them; teamwork clears path without harming living trees or forest. 4. Picnic preparations; Papa's yellow rain cape and Pikachu cause mistaken identity; Bear apologizes and helps repair mix-up. 5. Sunny picnic and gentle return; friendship and satisfied daytime rest, not bedtime.

Chapters

Chapter 1 — The Upside-Down Gentleman

Bear took out the Master Ball.

Its purple top gleamed in the sunshine. The big white letter on it looked very important. Bear held it up, then looked at Pikachu.

“Would you like to come on an adventure?”

Pikachu looked at Bear. Then at Papa, who was still holding his hat like bread.

“Pika?”

“Yes,” Papa said. “The sandwich is coming too.”

Pikachu's ears tipped forward. He gave a bright little hop.

Bear tossed the Master Ball. A flash of light caught Pikachu, and the ball closed with a click. It settled into the grass.

Bear crouched beside it.

“I caught Pikachu.”

He said it quietly first, as if the words were something he needed to try on.

Then he stood up.

“I CAUGHT PIKACHU!”

Papa grinned. “You certainly did.”

Bear picked up the ball and opened it again straight away.

Light spilled onto the path. Pikachu appeared, shook his long ears, and looked around.

“Pika!”

“You can walk with us,” Bear said. “You don't have to stay inside.”

Pikachu trotted to the edge of the trail, sniffed a flower, and sneezed.

A tiny spark flickered between his red cheeks.

Papa's hat lifted in the breeze at exactly the same moment.

Bear stared. Then he laughed.

“Did you zap Papa's hat?”

Papa caught it. “I hope not. It's my only bread.”

Pikachu made a chirpy sound and held his tail high.

Bear's eyes brightened. “Target practice! Can we do electric lightning-bolt target practice?”

Papa looked along the trail. A wide stone clearing lay ahead, with a low stone back wall and no dry grass inside it. Beside it stood an old outdoor game board, its painted circles faded but still cheerful.

“We can make a Pokémon game there,” he said. “First we look at the space.”

They walked over together.

Bear set his pack down and took out his drawing pad. Papa checked the clearing while Bear drew a wobbly circle on one sheet.

“Target one.”

He drew two eyes inside the circle.

“That's looking at me,” Papa said.

“It's a target.”

“Very nosy target.”

Bear added a moustache.

Papa considered it. “Much more respectable.”

They clipped the drawing into the game board's frame. Papa moved Bear's pack and water well away from the target area. Then he and Bear stood together behind a painted line, with Pikachu in the clear space ahead.

“Only the target,” Bear told Pikachu. “Not Papa.”

“Excellent rule,” Papa said.

Pikachu planted his feet. His cheek pouches glimmered.

“Pika… chu!”

A little zigzag of yellow light snapped toward the board.

The drawing spun around on its clip.

The moustache was upside down.

Bear doubled over laughing.

“You turned him into an upside-down gentleman!”

Papa bent sideways to inspect it. “He seems quite pleased.”

Pikachu looked back at them, ears pricked, and gave a proud little squeak.

Bear drew a second target. This one had Papa's hat, enormous eyebrows, and a sandwich for a nose.

Papa put a hand against his chest.

“At last. A proper portrait.”

Bear laughed so hard his pencil made a crooked line all the way across the page.

He held it up for Pikachu to inspect.

Pikachu tilted his head one way. Then the other.

“Pika.”

“I agree,” Papa said. “The resemblance is remarkable.”

Bear clipped the new drawing in place.

“Ready for another?”

Pikachu's tail gave one eager flick.

Before the next turn, Bear offered Pikachu a drink from his own little dish. Pikachu lapped, then sat in the shade beside them.

“Break time,” Bear said.

Papa opened his pack. “Even the world's finest moustache-turner needs a break.”

Pikachu shut his eyes happily.

This was already a very good adventure.

Chapter 2 — One Target at a Time

After their drink, Bear made the target game harder.

Not bigger sparks. Smaller targets.

He drew three circles across one sheet: a large one, a medium one, and a tiny one wearing a tiny hat.

Papa leaned over the drawing.

“Why does that one get a hat?”

“It's the captain.”

“Of course.”

Bear clipped the sheet onto the board and returned to Papa behind the line.

“Big circle first!”

Pikachu's cheeks flashed. A little bolt made the big circle flap.

“Medium!”

Flap.

“Captain!”

Pikachu narrowed his eyes. His ears stood tall. He gave a much smaller spark.

The tiny hat twitched.

Bear threw both hands up. “You got the captain!”

Papa saluted the drawing. “A brave hat. We shall remember its wobble.”

Pikachu bounced once, delighted.

Then Bear began calling targets faster.

“Big! Little! Middle! No, little! Wait, big!”

Pikachu looked from circle to circle. His cheeks fizzed. He stopped and turned toward Bear with both ears slanting sideways.

“Pika?”

Bear opened his mouth to repeat all the instructions.

Papa held up one hand.

Bear stopped.

Papa breathed out slowly, like he was cooling a spoonful of soup. Bear copied him. Pikachu's cheeks settled.

“That was a lot of targets at once,” Papa said.

“I knew which one I meant.”

“Did Pikachu?”

Bear looked at the three circles.

“Oh.”

He crouched so he could see Pikachu's face without leaning into the game space.

“Sorry. One at a time.”

Pikachu gave a little nod.

Bear raised his hand. “This means stop. Even if we're excited.”

Papa raised his hand too.

Pikachu sat down immediately.

Bear grinned. “Perfect stop.”

They practised it twice without sparks. Bear pointed, waited until Pikachu looked, and gave one clear instruction. Then he raised his hand and everyone paused.

Papa was exceptionally good at standing still.

Until a fly landed on his nose.

His nose twitched.

Bear's mouth trembled.

Papa crossed his eyes trying to see it.

Bear and Pikachu both burst into noise.

“I am a professional statue,” Papa said, very solemnly. “Please respect my work.”

They did not respect his work at all.

At the end, Bear unclipped his drawings and packed them away. The paper captain went on top, still wearing its crooked hat. Papa checked that the clearing was as tidy as they had found it.

Pikachu trotted beside Bear as they returned to the trail.

“Want to be on my team?” Bear asked.

Pikachu bumped his shoulder gently against Bear's leg.

“Pika!”

Papa checked the map. Ahead lay a little picnic meadow with a shallow stream running along its edge. Their lunch was in his pack. There were sandwiches. Real ones.

“I should warn you,” he said. “I packed cheese.”

Bear gasped. “The sandwich found it!”

Papa's sideways sandwich walk returned for three magnificent steps.

Pikachu tried to copy him.

His tail made the sideways shuffle look even sillier.

Bear laughed until he had to put his hands on his knees.

Then, around the next bend, the trail stopped.

Not really stopped. It was still there. But a tree lay across it.

The trunk stretched from one stone border to the other. Its roots had tipped up from a patch of ground farther away, leaving the rest of the trees standing tall and green.

Bear slowed.

Pikachu slowed too.

Papa put his map away and walked ahead to look, keeping them back from the fallen branches.

“Already down,” he said. “Nobody underneath. That's good.”

Bear pointed past the trunk. Through the branches he could see the picnic meadow, sunny and inviting.

“Our lunch place is over there.”

“So it is.”

“Could we climb it?”

Papa checked the loose branches and shook his head. “Not this one.”

Pikachu stood beside Bear. A little glow rose in his cheeks.

Bear looked at him, then at the fallen tree.

“What if Pikachu helps?”

Papa didn't answer straight away. He walked back to them, studied the wide stone path, and looked at the water flask clipped to his pack.

Bear had an idea growing. He could almost feel it pushing his eyebrows up.

“Target practice,” he said. “But for a job.”

Pikachu looked from Bear to Papa.

Bear opened his drawing pad on a flat stone. He sketched the trail as two long lines, with the fallen trunk across them.

“There's us,” he said, drawing three circles behind it.

Papa pointed at the smallest circle. “Which one is that?”

“Pikachu.”

“And the one with the enormous nose?”

“You.”

Papa nodded. “A very accurate map.”

Pikachu peered at his circle and touched it with one little paw. Bear added two long ears. That seemed to meet with approval.

All three of them stood still for a moment, thinking.

Chapter 3 — Just Enough of a Gap

“We could zap the tree,” Bear said. “And I have water.”

Papa sat beside the map stone instead of hurrying ahead.

“That is a Pokémon idea,” he said. “Not a thing we would try with a real fire in a real wood.”

Bear nodded. This was a day with a purple Master Ball in his pocket and a yellow friend who stored lightning in his cheeks. It was already quite different from an ordinary walk.

Pikachu peered at the drawing again.

Papa pointed toward the fallen tree. “We don't need to get rid of a whole tree. We only need to get past.”

Bear looked up.

“Oh. A little gap.”

“A walking gap.”

“Not a Papa-doing-a-sandwich-walk gap?”

“Those require enormous engineering.”

Bear giggled, then looked carefully along the trunk. Its far end rested on a bare stone patch, well away from the standing trees. A tangle of small fallen limbs covered the path there.

Papa went to check that end while Bear and Pikachu stayed at the map stone.

They watched him walk around it, never underneath. He tested nothing by jumping on it. He simply looked, took his time, and came back.

“The end is resting firmly on stone,” he said. “We can work on that little fallen tangle. The rest stays where it is.”

Bear drew a short gap at the edge of his map.

Pikachu touched the gap with a paw.

“That's it,” Bear said. “That bit. Not the trees. Not the flowers. Not Papa.”

“Still an excellent rule,” Papa said.

Papa took charge of the work area and the water. Bear's place was beside the map stone, where he could see Pikachu and call one clear target at a time. The lunch packs stayed behind them.

Before anything began, Papa raised his hand.

Pikachu stopped.

Bear raised his too.

Papa smiled. “Everyone knows the pause.”

Then he looked at Pikachu. “Ready to help?”

Pikachu's ears rose. His tail lifted. He gave a firm, cheerful nod.

Bear pointed to the small fallen tangle.

“Little spark. That bit.”

A bright thread leapt from Pikachu's cheeks.

It was nothing like a grown-up storm. It was a playful, impossible Pokémon zigzag, small enough to look as though someone had drawn it in the air with a yellow crayon.

It touched the tangle.

Fwoop.

A little patch glowed gold and crumbled into soft gray flecks on the stone.

Bear's mouth fell open.

“You did it!”

He began pointing toward another spot before Pikachu had finished looking back.

“And that one, and that one, and—”

Papa raised his hand.

Bear stopped.

Pikachu stopped.

For one second all Bear could hear was his own breathing and the distant stream.

Then Papa made his soup-cooling breath.

Bear copied him.

“One at a time,” Bear said, remembering.

Papa nodded. “We're not in a race.”

Pikachu's ears settled. Bear waited until Pikachu looked toward the next little target.

“This one.”

Another tiny gold flicker. Another soft fwoop.

Papa settled the glow with the water. It faded into a little puff of storybook steam, shaped so briefly like a rabbit that Bear wasn't sure whether he had imagined the ears.

“Did you see that?”

“Possibly a rabbit,” Papa said. “Possibly a very surprised teapot.”

Pikachu gave a delighted chirp.

They paused again. Papa checked the stone. Pikachu had a drink. Bear sat on his hands to stop them from pointing at everything at once.

That helped.

Mostly.

There was now a clear patch of path, but not enough to walk through. A crooked limb remained across the middle like a bent elbow.

Bear leaned sideways, trying to see around it.

Pikachu leaned sideways too.

Papa leaned sideways last.

For a moment they looked like three curious carrots in a breeze.

Bear snorted.

Papa looked at him gravely. “Serious inspection.”

That made Bear snort again.

Then he noticed something useful.

“Pikachu can't see the bottom bit from there.”

Pikachu looked back at him. One ear tilted.

Bear pointed to his map. “There's a sticking-out branch in front.”

Papa checked. “You're right.”

Bear almost said, Zap through it.

Instead he thought about the tiny captain's hat at the game board. Smaller target. Better view.

“What if we move where Pikachu stands?”

“Let's look first,” Papa said.

They paused the work completely. Papa found another clear place to stand, still facing the stone and away from the living trees. Pikachu walked there with him. Bear stayed at his own marker until Papa gave the all-clear.

Now the crooked limb was in plain view.

Pikachu looked at Bear, then at Papa.

Bear waited.

“Ready?”

“Ready,” Papa said.

“Pika!”

Bear pointed once.

The spark struck the fallen limb. A small gold shimmer ran along it, and the obstruction broke into harmless, dusty pieces. Papa's water put the last glimmer out.

The way through opened.

Bear sprang to his feet.

“We made a gap!”

He started forward, then stopped himself behind the marker.

Papa was still checking.

Waiting was harder when the picnic meadow was right there. Bear could see a yellow flower nodding on the other side. He could see the blue edge of the stream. He could almost taste his sandwich.

Pikachu sat beside him.

Bear sat too.

“You can wait really well,” he told Pikachu.

Pikachu's ears twitched toward the lunch pack.

“Except for lunch,” Bear added.

Papa came back with a smile.

“All finished. Cool, clear, and safe to pass. The fallen trunk is staying off to the side. It can be a home for little insects.”

Bear studied it. Most of the tree was still there, rough bark and all.

They hadn't needed to make everything vanish.

Just enough.

Bear held his hand out low. Pikachu touched it gently with a paw, his cheeks quiet.

“Team,” Bear said.

“Pika.”

Papa offered his hand too.

Bear put his other hand on top of Papa's. For a moment, their hands and Pikachu's little paw made a funny, warm pile.

“Sandwich team,” Papa said.

Bear laughed so suddenly the pile fell apart.

They crossed the cleared bit together, Papa first to show the path, Bear next, and Pikachu trotting at his side. The stream sounded louder now. Sunshine warmed the meadow.

At the far side, Bear looked back once.

Their map stone was still behind the tree. Papa fetched the drawing, checked that nothing had been left, and tucked it safely into Bear's pad.

Bear added a small star beside Pikachu's circle.

Then he added a star beside Papa's enormous nose.

“Water helper,” he explained.

Papa bowed. “An honour.”

“And me?” Bear said, looking at his own circle.

“What did you do?” Papa asked.

Bear thought.

“I watched. I stopped when you said. I found the place Pikachu couldn't see.”

He drew himself a star too.

Pikachu peered at the page and gave an approving chirp.

Papa put the pad away.

“Now,” he said, “I believe there is a matter of some missing cheese.”

Bear took Pikachu's little dish from the pack and hurried toward the picnic blanket.

The path was open.

The trees were quiet.

And lunch was finally ready to be found.

Pikachu reached the blanket first, but waited beside it until Bear caught up. Bear set down the dish. This time he remembered to ask his little teammate what he wanted before opening all the food at once.

Chapter 4 — Which Yellow Friend?

Pikachu wanted a berry.

He made this very clear by looking at the berry box, then at Bear, then at the berry box again.

“You're good at that target,” Bear said, opening it.

Papa spread the picnic blanket in the shade. A few drops fell from the leaves above, left over from a morning shower. One landed neatly on his head.

He looked up.

Another drop landed on his nose.

“The tree is doing target practice now,” Bear said.

Papa took a folded yellow rain cape from his pack and draped it over his shoulders while he moved their things to a drier spot. Its little hood sat up in two points around his ears.

Pikachu watched, nibbling.

Bear went to wash his hands with Papa's help, then dried them on the towel. When he came back, the blanket had shifted, the lunch boxes were open, and a yellow shape sat beside the pack.

Bear was looking into the sandwich box, not at the yellow shape.

“Papa, can you open this?” he asked, holding out a little tub.

“Pika?”

Bear nodded without looking.

“Yes, the lid. It's stuck.”

There was a pause.

A small paw rested on the tub.

Bear pushed it a little closer.

“Papa, you're doing it with the wrong hand.”

“Pika.”

Bear finally looked.

Two black-tipped ears.

Two red cheeks.

One very puzzled Pikachu.

Bear blinked.

Then he looked over his shoulder.

Papa stood beside the stream, still in his yellow cape, filling their dish from the clean water he'd brought for drinking.

“Oh.”

Pikachu looked down at the tub, then back at Bear.

Bear began to laugh.

“I thought you were Papa!”

Papa turned. “Was I very small?”

“Very.”

“And did I suddenly have a tail?”

“Very much.”

Papa considered this. “I should pay more attention.”

Bear laughed again, then noticed that Pikachu wasn't laughing with him yet. Pikachu was still holding the tub with both little paws, trying to understand the job he'd been given.

Bear sat on the blanket, leaving space beside him.

“Sorry, Pikachu. I wasn't looking properly. I thought you were Papa.”

Pikachu's ears lifted a little.

“And I sounded cross about the lid. You didn't do anything wrong.”

Bear took the tub back gently.

Pikachu let go.

For a moment they simply looked at each other. Bear didn't rush to fill the quiet. He remembered how confusing it had been when he'd called three targets at once.

Then he gave a small smile.

“Would you like to help me with lunch instead? A job for you.”

Pikachu chirped.

Bear put three soft napkins on the blanket.

“One for Papa. One for me. One for Pikachu.”

Pikachu touched his own napkin, then nudged it into a tidy square.

Papa came back and sat at the edge of the blanket.

“I shall open the lid,” he announced. “Being the one with the big hands.”

He opened it.

Inside were apple slices.

Bear stared at them.

“All that trouble for an apple.”

“A very well-protected apple,” Papa said.

Pikachu gave one sudden, bright chirp that sounded so much like laughter that Bear couldn't help joining in.

Papa lifted a sandwich.

“Now. Before lunch, identity check.”

Bear pointed at him. “Papa.”

He pointed at Pikachu. “Pikachu.”

He pointed at the sandwich.

There was a long pause.

“Papa?” Bear tried.

Papa looked deeply offended on the sandwich's behalf.

Pikachu tipped sideways in a happy little wobble.

This time all three of them were laughing together.

Then the breeze lifted a napkin.

It floated across the blanket and settled right on Pikachu's head.

For a moment, his ears poked through the folds like two sticks holding up a tiny tent.

Bear reached to pull it off, then stopped and looked at Pikachu first.

“Want some help?”

“Pika!”

Bear lifted the napkin away.

“There.”

Pikachu shook his ears and leaned against Bear's knee, his cheeks quiet and warm.

Papa folded the spare corner of the blanket under their lunch boxes so the breeze couldn't lift it.

“Team lunch,” Bear said.

“A fine institution,” Papa agreed.

They ate slowly. Bear offered Pikachu a berry and waited for him to take it. Papa passed the apple tub. Pikachu sniffed the sandwich crust with great interest, then returned to his own food.

Bear opened his drawing pad once more.

He turned to a clean page and drew Papa in the yellow cape. Beside him he drew Pikachu. He made both of Pikachu's ears very long.

Under Papa he wrote a large P.

Under Pikachu he began another P.

Then he stopped.

“This isn't helping.”

Papa leaned over.

“An unfortunate flaw in the alphabet.”

Bear thought, tongue pressed lightly against his lip. Then he drew a little hat beside Papa and a little lightning bolt beside Pikachu.

“There. Different pictures.”

Pikachu inspected his lightning bolt and chirped.

Papa inspected his hat. “No sandwich?”

Bear added one.

“Better,” Papa said.

Bear placed the drawing where they could all see it without the wind taking it away. Then he put his pencil down and handed Pikachu another berry.

“Sorry again about thinking you were Papa.”

Pikachu touched Bear's hand gently with his paw.

The mix-up was finished.

The lunch was not.

That was a very pleasing arrangement.

Bear settled back with an apple slice. Beside him, Pikachu put both paws on his carefully folded napkin and surveyed the picnic like a very small, very satisfied captain.

Chapter 5 — A Very Convincing Disguise

When lunch was finished, they stayed on the blanket for a while.

Nobody needed to hurry.

A butterfly crossed the meadow, landing on a flower and folding its wings. Pikachu watched it without moving. Bear watched Pikachu. Papa leaned against his rolled-up jacket and watched both of them.

“I liked the target with the moustache,” Bear said.

“A distinguished gentleman,” Papa agreed.

“And the little captain.”

“A remarkable hat.”

Pikachu gave a soft chirp.

Bear opened the drawing pad, turning past the map and the two yellow friends. He found the first target, upside-down moustache and all, and put it between them.

Pikachu tipped his head to study it.

Papa tipped his head too.

Bear laughed, but more quietly now. His tummy felt pleasantly full. His legs felt like they had done a good day's walking, even though there was still plenty of sunshine.

He lay on his back and looked up through the leaves.

A little patch of sky showed between two branches. It looked almost like a lightning bolt.

He pointed it out to Pikachu, who looked up with him.

“Your picture,” Bear said.

Pikachu's tail rested on the blanket, still and comfortable.

Papa folded the yellow cape and put it away.

“Identity confusion reduced,” he announced.

Bear turned his head. “I'm still checking the sandwich.”

“The sandwich is gone.”

Bear looked at Papa's tummy.

Papa nodded gravely.

“A very convincing disguise.”

Their laughter returned for one small, warm burst.

After that, they packed together. Papa checked the lunch boxes. Bear collected every napkin. Pikachu nudged the empty berry box toward the pack and waited while Bear put it inside.

Bear held up the Master Ball.

“Would you like to ride in here or walk?”

Pikachu trotted two steps along the path, then looked back.

“Walking,” Bear said.

He put the ball away and joined his friend.

They returned along the trail, passing through the clear gap beside the fallen trunk. Papa checked it again before they crossed. Beyond it, everything looked ordinary: leaves, stones, the little bend where their walk had changed.

But Bear walked more slowly now. Sometimes he looked at Pikachu before speaking, making sure his friend was listening.

Sometimes Pikachu looked at him first.

They were getting good at it.

By the time Papa Bear Home came into view, the afternoon sun lay in long, golden patches across the deck.

Papa brought out cool drinks and a soft cushion. Bear spread his drawings on the table, with smooth stones to keep their corners down. Pikachu curled on the cushion beside Bear's chair.

No sparks were needed.

Bear rested one hand near Pikachu's paw.

“We did lots,” he said.

“We did,” Papa answered.

“And we laughed lots.”

“A very important part.”

Bear leaned back. The deck was warm beneath his shoes. Pikachu sighed happily. Papa sat close enough that their chairs touched.

The adventure was finished.

The afternoon still belonged to them.

A breeze moved gently through the garden. Bear could hear leaves brushing together, a bird calling from somewhere beyond the fence, and the small clink of Papa setting down his cup.

For a little while, they simply enjoyed it.
