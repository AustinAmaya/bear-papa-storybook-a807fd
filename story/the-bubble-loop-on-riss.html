The Bubble Loop on RISS

Bear and Papa help Mittens, Captain Bolts, and Wrenchley follow a loop of floating water and return every drop to the RISS recycler.

Preamble

The call arrived while Bear was brushing his teeth.

Captain Bolts filled Papa’s tablet screen. His screen-face wore a worried zigzag.

“RISS has bubbles,” he said. “Not soap bubbles. Water bubbles. Forty-three of them.”

Behind him, a silver blur floated past. Mittens spun gently, reached with one magnetic paw, and missed.

Bear put down his toothbrush. “Do you need bubble helpers?”

“Immediately,” said Captain Bolts.

Papa held up Bear’s pajamas. “Then our bubble helpers are traveling comfortably.”

Chapter 1 — Water in the Air

The RISS shuttle docked with a soft clunk. Bear and Papa floated through the round hatch wearing slippers and pajamas under their station vests.

Mittens met them upside down.

She gave two bright chirps, pushed from the ceiling, and landed neatly on Papa’s shoulder. Her green eyes blinked toward the main corridor.

Dozens of clear water balls drifted there. Some were marble-sized. One was as wide as Papa’s hand. Each one wobbled and shone with tiny upside-down reflections of the station.

“Beautiful,” Bear whispered.

“Beautifully inconvenient,” said Wrenchley. Three of his six arms held towels. The other three held more towels.

Captain Bolts explained. A recycling-system test pouch had opened before it reached the sealed test box. Nothing dangerous had leaked—only clean water—but the floating drops could sneak behind warm control panels.

“All panel covers are closed,” Papa said, checking the wall lights. “So we have time to think.”

Bear reached slowly for a small bubble.

It slipped around his fingers and broke into three smaller ones.

Mittens pounced. Her paw passed between them. The bubbles sailed in three different directions.

Mittens’s ears tilted sideways.

“I think chasing makes them scatter,” Bear said.

Papa nodded. “Then perhaps our first job isn’t catching.”

Bear watched a bubble drift past the same blue handle twice.

“Our first job is finding out where they’re going.”

Chapter 2 — Mittens Draws the Wind

Mittens stretched her silver whiskers toward the corridor. Tiny green lights traveled from their tips to her cheeks.

Her tail projector clicked on.

A pale map appeared across the wall: the long RISS hallway, the round observation dome, and curling lines that looped through both.

“Airflow,” Papa said. “Her whiskers can feel it.”

Mittens added bright dots to the map. Every dot followed the same wide loop.

Bear pointed. “The bubbles aren’t wandering. The fans carry them past us, around the dome, and back again.”

Captain Bolts raised one finger toward his big blue button.

“Could that stop every fan?” Bear asked.

“Yes.”

“Would we like that?”

Captain Bolts lowered his finger. “Not even slightly.”

They could safely turn down one corridor fan, but first they needed to reach its control. A cloud of bubbles floated between them and the panel.

Bear’s chest felt quick. The biggest bubble was coming closer, and every push seemed to make the smaller ones spread.

Papa placed one hand on a yellow rail. “I’m going to become very boring,” he said. He breathed in slowly, then out. “No chasing. No swatting. Just noticing.”

Bear held the rail and matched him.

The bubble cloud sailed past without touching them.

Mittens stayed perfectly still beside Bear. Her whiskers glowed. On the next pass, her tail drew a narrow green path through the cloud—a quiet gap that appeared every twelve seconds.

“There’s our doorway,” Bear said.

They waited. The gap arrived.

Bear gave one gentle push. Papa followed. Mittens glided behind them, and all three slipped through without breaking a single bubble.

At the panel, Bear turned the corridor fan to LOW.

The loop slowed. Every bubble began drifting toward the observation dome.

Wrenchley opened a soft mesh collector.

Then Mittens chirped sharply.

Inside the largest bubble, something silver was spinning.

Chapter 3 — The Bubble with a Secret

Mittens enlarged the picture from her green eyes. The silver object appeared on the dome wall.

Wrenchley’s kazoo voice squeaked. “The recycling calibrator bead!”

Without that bead, the test system could not measure how much water it had recovered. The station was safe, but the recycler would remain paused—and nobody wanted to live forever on emergency juice boxes.

Captain Bolts’s face displayed a very serious juice box.

The huge bubble floated into the observation dome. Earth shone behind it, blue and white. The little silver bead rolled around inside the water like a moon inside a tiny glass planet.

Wrenchley held up the mesh collector.

“If we catch it too quickly, the bubble may burst,” Papa said. “Then the bead could bounce away.”

“If we move too slowly, it goes around the loop again,” said Bear.

Mittens planted her magnetic paws on a floor strip and stretched toward the bubble. Her paw pads hummed. The bead inside twitched.

The whole bubble wobbled toward her.

Bear’s eyes widened. “The magnet pulls the bead. The bead pulls the water!”

Papa smiled. “Surface tension is holding the bubble together. That gives us a handle on something we cannot hold.”

Wrenchley anchored the mesh collector beside Mittens. Bear held its soft rim open while Papa clipped the drain tube underneath.

The bubble drifted closer.

Too fast.

Its surface trembled. Smaller drops shook loose and sparkled around it.

Mittens’s green eyes grew wide.

“Easy,” Papa said softly. He loosened his shoulders and took one long breath. Bear did the same. Mittens lowered her magnetic strength; the hum became a purr.

The bubble slowed.

Bear watched its reflection cross one stripe on the floor. Then another.

“Now,” he said.

Mittens shifted one paw sideways. The bead rolled within the bubble. The bubble followed, curving neatly into the center of the mesh.

Wrenchley folded the collector closed.

Splish.

The great bubble became ordinary water inside the bag. The silver bead clicked safely against the bottom.

Captain Bolts bounced once. “Caught!”

That bounce sent three little bubbles toward his screen-face.

He froze.

The bubbles stopped a whisker away and drifted upward.

“Celebration later,” Bear said.

Captain Bolts nodded very carefully.

Wrenchley connected the drain tube. Water flowed back into the recycling tank, and the recovered bead slid into its padded slot.

The test panel lit green.

Forty-two bubbles remained.

Bear looked at Mittens. Mittens looked at Bear.

Her tail projected a picture of the mesh collector, followed by many tiny dots moving politely into it.

“That,” Bear said, “is an excellent plan.”

Chapter 4 — The Last Three Bubbles

With the corridor fan on LOW, the roundup became almost peaceful.

Mittens mapped each bubble’s path. Bear and Papa waited beside the green line. Wrenchley held the collector open. One by one, the shining drops floated into the mesh.

Captain Bolts counted without bouncing.

“Thirty-nine. Forty. Forty-one.”

His screen-face became a question mark.

Only one bubble remained on Mittens’s map. But the original count had been forty-three.

“Two are hiding,” Bear said.

Mittens swept her whiskers through the air. Her tail projected the airflow map again. Near the hydroponic garden, two tiny green curls appeared where the air barely moved.

They found one bubble trembling beneath a lettuce leaf. Another rested between the curls of a star-vine. Fans would scatter them, and the mesh was too large.

Papa held a dry drinking tube beside the first bubble. Bear placed one finger over the far end.

“When it touches, lift your finger,” Papa said.

The bubble kissed the tube. Bear lifted his finger. The drop slipped gently inside.

Mittens handled the star-vine bubble. She balanced on a rail, extended one careful paw, and touched the leaf—not the water. The leaf bent. The bubble rolled along its edge and dropped into Wrenchley’s smallest sample cup.

“Forty-two,” Captain Bolts announced.

The final bubble was still missing.

Mittens chirped. Everyone looked at her.

A marble-sized globe sat directly on the tip of her silver nose.

Bear covered his mouth, but a laugh escaped anyway.

Mittens went cross-eyed at the bubble. Her tail projected a large, unhappy exclamation mark.

Papa floated the sample cup beneath her chin. “One tiny nod.”

Mittens nodded.

Plip.

“Forty-three!” cried Captain Bolts.

This time, he celebrated by changing his screen-face into fireworks while keeping the rest of himself completely still.

The corridor was dry. The recycler hummed. Earth turned quietly beyond the dome.

Wrenchley presented Bear with a round blue sticker: ORBITAL BUBBLE WRANGLER.

He gave Papa one that said: CERTIFIED BORING—IN A HELPFUL WAY.

Papa examined it. “My finest award.”

Chapter 5 — One Glass of Water

The shuttle brought Bear and Papa home beneath a sky full of small, steady stars.

In Bear’s room, Papa tucked the blanket around him. The blue bubble-wrangler sticker rested on the bedside table beside the telescope.

Bear took a sip from his water glass.

“It stays at the bottom here,” he said.

“Gravity is very tidy that way,” Papa replied.

Bear imagined forty-three shining worlds floating through RISS. He imagined Mittens’s bright whiskers drawing invisible wind, and her careful magnetic paw pulling a moon through water.

“We didn’t have to be faster than the bubbles,” Bear murmured.

“Nope.”

“We had to see the loop.”

Papa smiled. “And become extremely boring.”

Bear giggled into his pillow.

Papa switched off the lamp. Moonlight rested on the telescope and turned the sticker silver-blue.

Far above, RISS crossed the night as one quiet point of light. Mittens was safe in her charging nest. The recycler was humming. Every drop of water was clipped, cupped, or tucked where it belonged.

Bear closed his eyes.

The room was still. Papa was close.

And nothing floated away.
