Bear, Papa, and Commander Aero — The Brave Team

Commander Aero explains flu vaccines, and brave Bear holds nervous Papa’s hand through a small but important adventure.

Preamble

Papa checked the appointment card again.

“Flu vaccines. Both of us.”

Bear pulled on his jacket. “That's the shot one.”

“Yes,” Papa said. He put his jacket on backwards.

Bear looked at the hood covering Papa's chin.

“Are you nervous?”

“A little,” Papa admitted, turning it around. He took one slow breath.

Commander Aero waited by the door, her wing-pack neatly folded.

“We can ask questions, make a plan, and take our time,” she said.

Bear reached for Papa's hand.

Papa smiled down at him.

“Good. My jacket and I could both use a plan.”

Three story seeds.

First, what does Bear ask Commander Aero about how flu vaccines help the body?

Second, what brave, comforting thing does Bear do to help nervous Papa through his shot?

Third, what funny little mix-up happens afterward, and how do they sort it out?

Chapter 1 — The Getting-It Part

The clinic had a fish tank in the waiting room.

One orange fish hovered beside a tiny castle as though it had forgotten why it had come downstairs.

Bear watched it while Papa checked them in.

Commander Aero sat beside him, her wing-pack folded clear of the next chair.

“Does the fish get a shot?” Bear asked.

“Not on today's appointment list,” she said.

Papa returned with two forms.

He sat down, stood up to fetch a pen, then sat down with the pen he had already been holding.

Bear noticed.

Papa noticed Bear noticing.

“My thoughts are doing a little running about,” Papa said.

He put both feet on the floor and let out a slow breath.

Bear put his feet down too. One shoe made a squeak.

Papa looked at it.

“An excellent contribution.”

Bear smiled. Then he turned to Commander Aero.

“What do flu vaccines do to your body?”

Commander Aero rested her tablet on her knees.

“Your body has a defence team,” she said. “It learns to recognise germs and deal with them. A vaccine helps it practise recognising flu.”

“Before flu comes?”

“Exactly.”

Bear thought about that.

“Like looking at a picture before you have to find something?”

“That's a useful way to think about it,” Commander Aero said.

Papa nodded. He knew about vaccines. Knowing did not quite stop his stomach from feeling wobbly.

“I like what the vaccine does,” he said. “I don't much like the getting-it part.”

“Both can be true,” Commander Aero said.

Bear looked at the little castle again. The fish had finally gone through its doorway.

“Can we have a plan for the getting-it part?”

“We certainly can,” Papa said.

A nurse came to the doorway and welcomed them.

Papa took Bear's hand, as usual.

Bear gave his hand a little squeeze, which was new.

Then the three of them followed the nurse into a bright room with a blue chair and a basket of stickers.

Papa glanced at the chair.

Bear glanced at the stickers.

Commander Aero glanced at both of them and smiled.

“Good team,” she said. “One step at a time.”

Chapter 2 — The Practice-Shield

The nurse checked their information and asked Papa a few questions. Then she explained what would happen.

“A quick pinch,” she said. “Your arm might feel sore afterward.”

Bear liked that she said what she meant.

Not a surprise.

Not a trick.

Commander Aero opened a blank page on her tablet and drew a round shape with little bumps.

“A pretend picture of a flu virus,” she said.

Bear pointed at it. “It looks like a potato wearing buttons.”

Papa leaned over. “A dreadful fashion decision.”

Commander Aero drew a few small shapes beside it.

“The flu shot gives your defence team harmless clues about flu. Those clues help your body learn to make antibodies.”

“What's an antibody?” Bear asked.

“A little helper your body makes that can recognise and stick to particular germs. Your body also keeps a memory of what it practised.”

Bear tapped his own forehead.

“In here?”

“Not the kind of memory you use for your favourite song,” Papa said. “Your immune cells do the remembering.”

Bear studied the button potato.

“So the shot isn't giving me the flu?”

“No,” the nurse said. “The flu shot cannot give you flu.”

Commander Aero drew a little shield.

“It helps lower your chance of getting flu. And if you do catch it, it can help keep you from getting so sick.”

“Not an everything-shield?”

“Not an everything-shield,” Commander Aero agreed.

Papa looked at the picture. “A useful practice-shield.”

Bear nodded.

That sounded worth doing.

His tummy still gave a little flip when the nurse moved the supplies closer.

“Can I look at Commander Aero's picture instead of my arm?” Bear asked. “And I want to hold Papa's hand while you do it.”

“Of course you can,” the nurse said. “We'll get you comfortable before we start.”

Papa sat beside him, close and steady.

“Let's try our slow breath,” he said.

He breathed in gently, then let the air out slowly.

Bear copied him. Commander Aero moved the tablet where he could see it without turning his shoulder.

“Would you like a warning, or would you rather keep talking and look away?” the nurse asked.

“Please don't tell me when you're going to do it,” Bear said. “I don't want to wait for the pinch.”

“No countdown or warning, then. Once you're settled and have said I can go ahead, I'll follow that plan.”

Bear nodded. “Okay. You can go ahead while we talk about the picture.”

Papa squeezed his hand softly.

Commander Aero added one enormous button to the potato picture.

Bear smiled.

“That one is its belly button.”

“Scientifically questionable,” Papa said.

“But funny,” Commander Aero replied.

Bear settled his arm where the nurse showed him. His hand stayed tucked safely in Papa's.

He was ready to try.

Chapter 3 — It's Okay

The nurse cleaned a little patch of Bear's arm.

“Ooh, that feels cold,” Bear said, turning toward the tablet.

Papa's hand felt warm around his. Papa breathed slowly, and Bear joined him.

“Do you think that potato picked out its own hat?” Papa asked.

“No, because it hasn't got any—ow.”

“All done, Bear,” the nurse said. She had followed his plan, without a countdown or a warning.

He blinked.

A little plaster covered the place already.

“It did pinch,” he said.

“It did,” Papa agreed. “You told us what helped, and you did it.”

Commander Aero gave him a quiet cheer.

“Well done, Bear.”

Bear inspected his plaster. His arm felt different, but he was still himself. Same jacket. Same shoes. Same Papa.

Then Papa looked at the blue chair.

His smile became a little stiff.

“My turn,” he said.

He sat down carefully and put both feet on the floor.

Bear moved to the chair beside him.

Papa began rolling up his sleeve. It caught halfway. He tugged too quickly, then stopped and loosened the fold.

“I am more nervous than I expected,” he said.

The nurse waited.

“Thank you for telling me. We can take a moment.”

Papa nodded. He let his shoulders drop and breathed slowly out.

Bear watched his face.

Papa was big. Papa knew how engines worked. Papa could open the difficult jars.

And Papa was nervous about a shot.

Bear thought about how Papa's hand had felt when it was his turn.

He held out his own.

“Would you like this?”

Papa put his free hand in Bear's.

“Yes, please.”

Bear curled his fingers around it.

“It's okay,” he said. “It's okay.”

Papa looked down at him.

“Thank you.”

“Do you want to look at the potato?” Bear asked.

“Very much.”

Commander Aero shifted the tablet. The button potato now had a small hat.

“Appropriate waiting-room clothing,” she said.

Papa gave a little laugh. It sounded much more like Papa.

The nurse asked what would help him.

“I'd like to stay sitting and look away,” Papa said. “And please don't tell me when the shot is coming. No countdown, either. I'd rather keep talking with Bear.”

“That's fine. You let me know when you're comfortable and I have your permission to go ahead.”

Papa settled his arm and looked toward Bear. “I'm comfortable now. You can go ahead while we talk.”

Bear kept hold of his hand.

Papa was not squeezing too hard. His fingers were simply there, warm and a little tense.

“It's okay,” Bear said again.

Commander Aero's voice stayed calm.

“You've made your plan. We're right here.”

Papa looked at the tablet and let out another slow breath.

The nurse cleaned his arm.

Papa's eyebrows went up.

“You weren't kidding about the cold bit,” he told Bear.

“I know! But look at me, Papa. We can keep talking.”

Bear watched Papa's face instead of his arm. The nurse followed their agreed plan, with no warning about the timing.

“It's okay,” Bear said softly. “It's okay. I'm right here with you.”

Papa breathed out. His fingers tightened for one small moment.

Then the nurse said, “Finished.”

Papa looked back.

“Already?”

“Already.”

Commander Aero brought her hands together in a gentle clap.

“Brilliant teamwork. Both of you.”

Bear grinned so widely his cheeks felt round.

Papa looked at his little plaster, then at Bear.

“You helped me feel less alone with the nervous part,” he said.

Bear leaned close, careful of both their arms.

“You do that for me.”

“I do,” Papa said. “And the nurse took good care of us both.”

The nurse smiled and offered the sticker basket.

Bear chose a blue star.

Papa considered the basket with great seriousness.

“May grown-ups have one?”

“Certainly.”

He chose a tiny smiling pear.

Bear stared at it.

“Not a potato?”

“I am expanding my interests.”

They sat together for the clinic's waiting time while the nurse explained what they might notice later and when to ask for help.

Bear listened. Papa listened. Commander Aero tucked away the tablet.

The appointment card lay on Papa's knee.

Two vaccines.

Both finished.

Bear rested his star-stickered hand beside Papa's pear.

For once, Papa wasn't checking the card at all.

Chapter 4 — The Electronical Shot

When their waiting time was finished and the nurse said they could go, Bear stood beside Commander Aero.

He looked at her sleeve.

Then at the blue chair.

Then back at her sleeve.

“Your turn.”

Commander Aero tilted her head.

“My turn?”

“For your electronical shot.”

Papa paused with one arm in his jacket.

“An electronical shot?”

Bear nodded.

“Because we're getting our shots. Commander Aero needs hers too.”

It seemed very sensible. You could not have a brave team and leave one of the team out.

Bear pulled the little chair nearer, leaving plenty of space around the nurse's supplies.

“I can hold your hand as well,” he offered.

Commander Aero's expression softened.

“That is a very kind offer. But I've already had all my shots.”

Bear stopped moving the chair.

“All of them?”

“All the ones I need. I'm up to date.”

Papa finished putting on his jacket.

The right way round this time.

“Today's appointment is just for us,” he said.

Bear looked slightly disappointed.

He had become good at being the hand-holder. He had another whole hand available.

Commander Aero sat beside him for a moment.

“You needn't find me a shot to include me,” she said. “I came to cheer you on.”

“Oh.”

“And you did,” Papa said. “Very well.”

Bear considered this.

“Not an extra shot just for joining in?”

“No extra shot just for joining in,” Commander Aero agreed.

Papa checked the floor for their things.

“I would like no extra anything that involves rolling up my sleeve.”

Bear laughed.

Then he took the remaining small paper star from his pocket. He had folded it while they waited, and one point was much shorter than the others.

“Could you have this instead?”

Commander Aero held out her hand.

“I'd be delighted.”

Bear placed it there.

“Cheering-on star.”

She studied its uneven points as seriously as she might study a new machine.

“Excellent design. One short point, so I can always tell which one it is.”

Bear beamed.

He pushed the chair back where it belonged and thanked the nurse.

At the doorway, Papa checked for his keys.

Then his appointment card.

Then his pen.

“It was in your hand before,” Bear reminded him.

Papa looked down.

There it was again.

Commander Aero held her paper star against her tablet.

“Equipment check complete.”

They walked back past the fish tank.

The orange fish was outside its tiny castle now, hanging quietly in the water.

Bear gave it a small wave.

“No shot for you today,” he told it. “And no electronical one for Commander Aero.”

Papa leaned down.

“And no more jackets on backwards.”

“That's not a shot,” Bear said.

“No,” Papa replied. “That was a separate adventure.”

Chapter 5 — The Brave Team Rests

At Papa Bear Home, they hung their jackets on the hooks.

Papa took a moment to make sure his hood was at the back.

Bear checked too.

“Passed,” he said.

Commander Aero set her paper star beside her cup while Papa made warm drinks. Bear climbed onto his usual chair and placed his blue sticker next to the star.

Papa's smiling pear joined them.

“A distinguished gathering,” Commander Aero said.

Bear leaned against Papa.

“My arm's a bit sore.”

“Mine too,” Papa said. “We'll have a gentle evening.”

They followed the nurse's instructions and settled together with a picture book.

Bear turned the pages slowly. Papa read the voices. Commander Aero supplied an especially serious voice for a duck who had lost a boot.

By the end, Bear was resting his head against Papa's side.

“Were you still scared when I held your hand?” he asked.

“A little,” Papa said. “But holding your hand helped.”

Bear looked at their hands on the blanket.

“I was brave.”

“You were,” Papa said. “And so was I, even with my wobbly tummy.”

Commander Aero lifted her cup.

“To the brave team.”

Bear touched his cup gently to Papa's.

Later, Commander Aero said goodnight, taking her little paper star safely with her.

Papa helped Bear into his pyjamas and tucked him into bed. The room was warm. His jacket waited on its hook. The appointment card was put away.

Bear held out his hand once more.

Papa took it.

“It's okay,” Bear murmured.

Papa brushed the sandy fur above Bear's forehead.

“Yes,” he whispered. “It is.”

Bear's fingers relaxed.

Papa stayed beside him while the house grew quiet, and the brave team rested.
