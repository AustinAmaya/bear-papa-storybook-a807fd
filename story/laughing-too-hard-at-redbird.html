Laughing Too Hard at Redbird

Bear tells Papa a joke about a Minecraft creeper hiding in his chicken, sparking a giggly running bit — until a real, friendly creeper wanders out from behind the condiment counter, drawn by the smell of fries.

Preamble

Redbird smelled like fries and adventure, same as always.

Bear slid into their favorite window booth, already grinning. Papa slid in across from him, setting down a tray piled high with honey biscuits and crispy chicken.

"Mission tonight," Papa said, eyes twinkling. "See who can make the silliest face before the biscuits get cold."

Bear crossed his eyes and puffed out his cheeks. Papa answered with a face so ridiculous that Bear burst out laughing so hard he had to grab the table.

"Truce!" Bear wheezed. "I can't breathe!"

Papa was laughing too now, shoulders shaking, biscuit forgotten halfway to his mouth.

This was going to be one of those Redbird nights.

Chapter 1 — Creeper Chicken

Bear picked up a piece of crispy chicken, studied it very seriously, and turned to Papa with the straightest face he could manage.

"Papa," he said. "I think there's a creeper in this chicken."

Papa blinked. "A... creeper?"

"Yeah." Bear held up the piece dramatically. "Look how green it got when I wasn't looking. Very suspicious."

Papa squinted at the completely normal, completely brown piece of fried chicken. "That looks perfectly golden to me."

"That's exactly what a creeper would want you to think," Bear said, nodding gravely, "right before it says hsss."

He made the exact hissing sound a Minecraft creeper makes right before it explodes, and Papa nearly choked on his biscuit trying not to laugh.

"That," Papa sputtered, "is the silliest thing you have said all week."

"It's a very serious chicken emergency," Bear said, deadpan, and then completely ruined it by cracking up himself.

That set Papa off too. He laughed so hard he had to put his cup down before he spilled it, wiping his eyes with the back of his hand.

"Okay, okay," Papa said, still grinning. "So what do we do about this alleged creeper chicken?"

Bear considered this with mock seriousness, tapping his chin. "We monitor it. Very closely. For science."

"For science," Papa agreed solemnly, and picked up his own piece of chicken to inspect it just as dramatically, turning it this way and that under the restaurant lights.

"Yours looks clear," Bear reported. "Mine's still suspicious."

"Should we call it Steve?" Papa asked. "Every Minecraft chicken needs a name."

Bear gasped, delighted. "Steve the Creeper Chicken!"

They both dissolved into giggles so loud that the family in the next booth turned to look, smiling at the sound of it.

"You know," Papa said, once he'd caught his breath, "if there really were a creeper hiding somewhere in this restaurant, I bet it would smell the fries before it smelled us."

Bear's eyes lit up at the thought. "Ooh. Like a fry-seeking creeper."

"Exactly like that," Papa said, grinning, already reaching for another biscuit like the whole conversation was perfectly normal.

Chapter 2 — The Hissing Rule

By the time Bear's second biscuit arrived, he and Papa had turned the Creeper Chicken bit into a full investigation, complete with imaginary clipboards and very serious note-taking.

"Suspect: chicken tender number three," Bear announced, holding it up like evidence. "Motive: unknown. Behavior: suspiciously delicious."

Papa scribbled invisible notes in the air with his finger, nodding along. "Noted. Recommend immediate taste-testing to confirm threat level."

"For safety," Bear agreed, and ate the whole tender in one triumphant bite.

"Case closed," Papa declared. "That one was definitely not a creeper. Too tasty."

Bear giggled, reaching for a fry, when the restaurant's front door chimed and the manager walked out from the back carrying a fresh tray, chatting cheerfully with one of the cooks about the dinner rush.

"You know," Papa said, watching the kitchen door swing shut, "the manager here always says this place is a little bit magic."

Bear perked up. "Magic how?"

"He says sometimes things go missing from the kitchen and turn up somewhere strange," Papa said, keeping his voice mysterious and low, clearly enjoying himself. "Like the portal behind the ketchup bottles."

Bear's eyes went wide with delight, remembering. "The Minecraft portal!"

"The very same," Papa said, grinning.

Bear glanced toward the counter, toward the shelf of condiments, half-joking and half-genuinely curious. "Do you think Steve the Creeper Chicken came through there?"

"Anything's possible at Redbird," Papa said solemnly, and that was somehow the funniest thing either of them had said all night — they both cracked up again, laughing so hard that Bear had to set down his fry before he dropped it.

A family two booths over glanced over, smiling at the sound of their laughter filling the little restaurant.

"Okay, okay," Bear said, still hiccupping with giggles, "new rule. Every time somebody says 'creeper,' the other person has to make the hissing sound."

"That is a terrible rule," Papa said, already grinning at the thought of it, "and I love it completely."

"Creeper," Bear said immediately, testing him.

Papa let out the most dramatic hsss he could manage, throwing his hands up like he was about to explode, and Bear nearly slid right off the booth seat laughing.

"Creeper," Papa said back, just to make Bear do it.

Bear hissed so enthusiastically that a piece of fry went flying, and that somehow made everything even funnier — Papa laughing so hard tears were forming at the corners of his eyes.

Bear wiped his own eyes, catching his breath. "This is the best dinner ever."

"Every dinner with you is the best dinner ever," Papa said, reaching over to ruffle Bear's hair, and Bear beamed so wide his cheeks hurt.

Chapter 3 — A Real Creeper (Friendly!)

Bear was mid-hiss, hands thrown up in his best explosion pose, when something small and green shuffled out from behind the condiment counter.

He froze.

Papa froze too, biscuit halfway to his mouth.

It was small — smaller than Bear, blocky, and undeniably, unmistakably green. It bumped gently into a napkin dispenser, made a soft, confused little "hnn?" sound, and looked up at them with tiny black eyes.

"Papa," Bear whispered, eyes enormous. "That's not chicken."

"No," Papa agreed slowly, setting his biscuit down very carefully. "That's a real one."

The little creeper wobbled a step closer to their booth, sniffing hesitantly at the air, clearly drawn by the smell of fries drifting from their tray. It didn't hiss. It didn't charge. It just stood there, swaying slightly, looking a little lost and a lot hungry.

Bear's first instinct was pure joy — but right behind it came a flicker of nervousness, remembering everything he knew about creepers exploding.

He took a slow breath, the way Papa had taught him. In... out...

"Is it going to be okay?" Bear asked quietly.

Papa studied the little creeper for a long moment — the way it swayed, the way it hadn't puffed up or gone stiff, the calm, curious tilt of its head. "I think it's just confused," Papa said gently. "And hungry. Not angry. There's a difference."

Bear slid down carefully from the booth, moving slow, the way they always did around anything new and uncertain. He held out a single fry, arm steady, breath calm.

The little creeper leaned forward, sniffed the fry twice, and ate it right out of Bear's fingers with a happy little "mrrp" sound, wobbling with what looked almost like a wag.

Bear grinned so wide his whole face lit up. "Papa, it's friendly!"

"It certainly seems to be," Papa said, relaxing, a smile spreading across his face too. "Looks like Steve the Creeper Chicken turned out to be an actual creeper after all."

The little creeper made a soft, pleased sound and pressed its blocky head gently against Bear's knee, exactly like it wanted to say hello.

Papa reached slowly for the little science tablet tucked in his jacket pocket — the same one they always brought on trips like this — and tapped it awake.

"Just in case we need backup," he said quietly, keeping his movements slow so as not to startle their new visitor.

The creeper, meanwhile, had discovered the tray of fries properly and was now nudging one gently across the table with its blocky nose, clearly hoping for another handout.

"I don't think it wants backup," Bear whispered, grinning. "I think it wants fries."

"Probably both," Papa said, sliding one carefully across the table.

The creeper ate it happily, then hopped — an odd, bouncy little hop — onto the empty seat beside Bear, curling up there like it had found exactly the right spot.

Bear looked at Papa, delighted and a little amazed. "It picked us."

"It picked the fries," Papa said, smiling, "but I think it likes you too."

The manager came by a few minutes later to check on their table, and Bear held very still, worried for a second about what he'd think. But the manager just chuckled at the sight of the little green visitor curled up on the booth seat.

"Huh," he said, scratching his head, "that's a new one. Don't worry — happens sometimes around here. This place has always been a little bit magic."

Bear relaxed instantly, relieved, and gave the creeper a gentle pat between its stubby little arms.

"Should we help it find its way home?" Bear asked, already thinking ahead, one hand resting gently on the creeper's rounded green head.

"I think," Papa said, glancing toward the shelf of condiments and the shimmer he could just barely make out behind the ketchup bottles, "that's exactly what we should do."

Chapter 4 — Deep Breaths

Papa carried the little creeper carefully in both arms, cradling it against his chest as he and Bear made their way toward the condiment counter, right past the ketchup bottles where the faint shimmer waited.

"Ready?" Papa asked.

Bear nodded, though his heart was thumping with a mix of excitement and nerves — the good kind of nervous, but still a lot all at once.

The little creeper, sensing the shift in the air near the portal, started to wriggle and squirm, letting out a worried little "hnn-hnn-hnn" sound.

"It's okay," Bear said softly, reaching out to pat its blocky head. "It's okay, you're okay."

But his own hands were shaking a little too, and his breath was coming fast and shallow.

Papa noticed immediately. He crouched down to Bear's eye level, creeper still balanced gently in one arm.

"Hey," Papa said, calm and steady. "Let's do our breathing. Both of us."

Bear nodded, grateful, and together they breathed in slow through their noses — one, two, three — and let it out even slower.

In... out...

In... out...

The little creeper's wriggling slowed, almost like it could feel the calm spreading from Bear's hands to its own blocky little body. It let out a small, settling sigh.

"Better?" Papa asked.

"Better," Bear said, and it was true — his hands had steadied, his heart wasn't racing anymore, and the excited nervousness had turned back into simple excitement.

"Deep breaths fix almost everything," Bear said thoughtfully.

"Almost everything," Papa agreed, smiling. "Ready now?"

"Ready," Bear said, and this time his voice was steady too.

Papa held the creeper close to the shimmering patch of air behind the ketchup, and with the gentlest little push, the portal seemed to recognize its own, pulling the small green shape through with a soft, sparkly whoosh.

For just a second, right before it vanished completely, the little creeper turned back and made one last happy "mrrp," as if to say thank you and goodbye all at once.

Then it was gone, and the shimmer settled back into stillness, just an ordinary shelf of condiments once again.

Bear let out a long breath, this one entirely happy. "We did it."

"We did," Papa said, ruffling his hair. "Deep breaths, a little courage, and a lot of fries."

Bear laughed, the good, easy kind of laugh, no longer wound up but warm and settled instead.

They walked back to their booth together, and Bear noticed his own tray was still there, biscuits gone slightly cold, fries a little limp — but somehow it didn't matter at all.

"Best. Dinner. Ever," Bear said, flopping happily back into his seat.

"I believe you said that already tonight," Papa said, sliding in across from him, "but I'll allow the repeat. Tonight earned it twice over."

The manager wandered by once more, glancing curiously at the now-empty seat beside Bear. "Everything settle down over here?"

"All settled," Papa said easily. "Just a little Redbird magic, same as always."

The manager chuckled and nodded like that explained everything perfectly, and went back to refilling the soda machine.

Chapter 5 — Walking Home

By the time they finished dinner, the sky outside Redbird's big front window had turned soft and dark, streetlights glowing gold along the sidewalk.

Papa paid the bill while Bear folded his napkin into a tiny, lopsided crown, still smiling about the whole strange, wonderful evening.

"Best night," Bear said again, for what had to be the fifth time, as they walked out together into the cool evening air.

"Best night," Papa agreed, and meant it completely.

They walked home slowly, hand in hand, Bear recounting the whole thing all over again — the creeper chicken joke, the hissing rule, the real creeper's happy little "mrrp" — laughing softly at each part like it was brand new.

"Do you think Steve made it home okay?" Bear asked, looking up at the stars just beginning to appear.

"I think so," Papa said. "That portal seemed to know exactly where it was going."

Bear yawned, content and worn out in the good way, the kind of tired that only comes after laughing until your stomach hurts and then calming all the way back down again.

By the time they reached their own front step, Little Red Bear and Little Polar Bear were waiting in their usual spot, still and warm in the porch light.

"Evening, you two," Papa said to the statues, the way he always did, and Bear gave them each a gentle pat on the head on his way inside.

Upstairs, Bear brushed his teeth, put on his favorite pajamas, and climbed into bed feeling happy all the way down to his toes.

Papa tucked the blanket in close and sat on the edge of the bed for a moment, the way he always did.

"Thank you for the best dinner ever," Bear said sleepily.

"Thank you for the best company," Papa said back, smoothing Bear's hair. "Goodnight, silly chicken detective."

Bear giggled one last time, soft and sleepy. "Goodnight, Papa."

The room went quiet and warm, and Bear drifted off smiling, dreaming of fries and friendly creepers and the very best kind of ordinary Tuesday.
