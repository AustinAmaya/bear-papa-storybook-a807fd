The Missing THWUMP

When Cheetah's usual THWUMP goes missing, tiny pawprints, a magnifying glass, and a TOP SECRET machine lead Bear and Papa to her funniest surprise yet.

Preamble

Bear waited on the deck for Cheetah’s usual THWUMP.

Nothing thwumped.

Papa checked the yard. Bear checked behind the patio chair, even though Cheetah was much bigger than a patio chair.

Then Bear found a card beneath his cocoa cup.

TODAY’S GAME: FIND CHEETAH.

CLUE: I AM CLOSER THAN I LOOK.

A tiny giggle came from somewhere near the flowerpots.

Bear looked left. Papa looked right.

Another giggle came from the picnic table.

“That was fast,” Bear said.

“Very fast,” Papa agreed.

Neither of them noticed the object beside the card: a little silver machine with one button glowing green.

Chapter 1 — The Quietest Search

The tiny giggle stopped.

Bear waited for another.

Nothing.

His smile disappeared. “Papa, where is she?”

Cheetah was fast. Cheetah was silly. Cheetah occasionally arrived upside down in a shrub and claimed she had planned it.

But Cheetah was never quiet for this long.

Bear’s chest tightened. “What if something happened?”

Papa knelt until his light-brown eyes were level with Bear’s.

“I feel scared too,” he said. “So before our fear starts inventing answers, let’s help our bodies become good detectives.”

Papa put one hand on his belly and breathed in slowly.

Bear copied him.

In.

Out.

Again.

The deck did not change. But Bear’s thoughts stopped bumping into one another.

“What do we actually know?” Papa asked.

Bear picked up the card. “Cheetah made a game.”

“There are no signs that anyone was hurt.”

“We heard her giggle twice.”

“And the message says she is close.”

Bear nodded. He still felt scared. It simply was not driving the search by itself anymore.

“Cheetah!” he called. “Safety check! Are you okay?”

For one moment, the yard stayed silent.

Then a voice no louder than a squeaky toy called, “I’m okay! Still hiding!”

Bear and Papa looked at each other.

“That sounded like Cheetah,” Bear said.

“A very unusual amount of Cheetah,” Papa replied.

They began at the flowerpots. Bear checked behind each pot while Papa checked beneath the leaves without pulling or poking. No Cheetah.

At the picnic table, they found one cocoa crumb broken neatly in half.

“Cheetah likes snacks,” Bear said. “But she does not usually divide crumbs with laboratory precision.”

Beside the crumb lay a golden hair so short Bear nearly missed it.

They searched the deck boards, the chair legs, and the path to the yard. Every clue seemed strangely small.

Bear returned to the card.

I AM CLOSER THAN I LOOK.

He tilted it toward the light. Near the bottom edge was a dot the size of a pepper speck.

The dot moved.

Bear leaned closer.

It did not move again.

“I need bigger eyes,” he said.

Papa glanced toward the workshop where Bear’s magnifying glass waited in its case.

“Then bigger eyes are exactly what we’ll bring.”

Chapter 2 — Bigger Eyes

Bear carried the magnifying glass from the workshop in its padded case. Papa brought a small flashlight and a sheet of white paper.

“No giant nets?” Bear asked.

“No giant nets,” Papa said. “We are finding Cheetah, not collecting butterflies.”

They returned to the pepper-sized dot on the card.

Through the round lens, the paper fibers became thick white ropes. The printed letters had bumpy black edges. The dot came into focus last.

It was not a dot.

It was a pawprint.

Bear nearly dropped the glass.

The print had a heel pad and four tiny toe marks. It was smaller than a grain of rice.

“Cheetah,” he whispered.

From somewhere in the yard came a squeaky, delighted, “Warmer!”

Bear’s fear tried to jump up again. Papa put one hand on his own belly and breathed slowly. Bear joined him without being reminded.

“She says she is okay,” Bear said.

“And now we know why every clue seemed small,” Papa replied. “We do not know how yet.”

Bear moved the magnifying glass too quickly across the deck. Everything flashed blurry.

“I can’t find another print.”

Papa held the white paper beneath the lens. “A magnifying glass enlarges a small area. That helps—but it also means we see less of the whole scene at once.”

“So I search in a pattern.”

Bear began at the card and moved left to right in careful rows. Papa marked each checked section with a wooden counting block. Cheetah did not giggle. Apparently hiding also required professional seriousness.

At the deck’s edge, Bear found a second pawprint in a smudge of cocoa dust.

A third appeared beside it.

Then the trail vanished.

Papa aimed the flashlight low across the boards instead of straight down. Tiny dents cast long shadows.

“There!” Bear said.

The prints crossed the deck and reached the gap between two boards. A golden thread—one tiny strand of Cheetah’s fur—was looped around the edge like a climbing rope.

Bear followed it down to the grass. Dew had erased the marks below.

“That’s cheating,” he told the lawn.

The lawn did not apologize.

They searched around the nearest stepping stone. Hard stone held no prints. Soft dirt did.

Bear found one tiny track, then another, heading away from Papa Bear Home.

At the garden gate, the trail disappeared again beneath a crushed dandelion.

Papa lifted one yellow petal. Under it lay a piece of leaf clipped into the shape of an arrow.

The arrow pointed along the path toward Cheetah’s house.

Bear closed the magnifying-glass case around the clue so it would not blow away.

“She left us a whole trail,” he said.

Papa looked at the very ordinary path and the impossibly small arrow.

“Then we had better bring our bigger eyes with us.”

Chapter 3 — TOP SECRET

Bear and Papa followed the path toward Cheetah’s house.

To ordinary eyes, it was only a path.

Through the magnifying glass, it was a highway of clues.

Tiny pawprints crossed a patch of dust beside the curb. A golden hair clung to a blade of grass. One clover leaf had been folded into a little arrow.

Bear moved slowly, searching one small circle at a time.

“Print,” he said.

Papa marked the spot on his tablet.

“Hair.”

Another mark.

“Extremely tiny sandwich crumb.”

Papa examined it. “Cheese.”

“Definitely Cheetah.”

At Cheetah’s front walk, the pawprints became clearer. They passed between stones that must have looked like giant cliffs to someone so small. One print had slid sideways in a patch of pollen.

Bear imagined Cheetah skidding, popping up, and announcing that she meant to do it.

He almost laughed. Then he remembered how very small the print was.

Papa noticed his face. He breathed in slowly.

Bear joined him.

“We know she was safe when she answered,” Papa said. “Now we keep gathering facts.”

They reached the front door and knocked.

From inside came a squeak.

“Still hiding!”

Bear’s shoulders loosened.

The door was already open a finger-width. Bear and Papa entered carefully, checking the floor before every step.

Cheetah’s house looked normal from Bear’s height. The couch was normal. The rug was normal. A bowl containing seventeen snack crackers was completely normal for Cheetah.

The magnifying glass told a different story.

Tiny pawprints crossed the welcome mat, circled the cracker bowl twice, and continued under a worktable.

Bear lay on his belly several feet away and aimed the glass beneath it.

Dust became boulders. A loose thread became a rope. In the middle stood a silver device with a wide glass front, three dials, and a label in enormous red letters:

TOP SECRET

Beneath that, in smaller letters, someone had added:

ESPECIALLY FROM BEAR UNTIL THE SURPRISE IS READY

Bear looked over the lens at Papa.

“I think the surprise is ready.”

A giggle squeaked from behind the machine.

Bear returned his eye to the glass. The prints went around the left side, but no Cheetah appeared. He shifted the lens too quickly and lost focus.

Papa handed him the flashlight. “Low and slow.”

Bear placed the light on the floor. Its sideways beam cast long shadows from tiny objects.

There was a matchbox with wheels.

A bottle-cap table.

A cracker crumb on a plate made from a button.

And behind the button stood the smallest Cheetah Bear had ever seen.

She was shorter than his thumb. Her golden eyes gleamed. Her dark spots looked like pepper flakes. She wore a paper cape with a lightning bolt drawn on it.

Cheetah put both paws on her hips.

“Surprise!” she squeaked.

Bear stared.

Papa stared.

Cheetah attempted her usual dramatic leap from behind the button.

She landed on a folded tissue with the tiniest sound imaginable.

“Thwump,” she announced.

Bear burst out laughing.

Papa laughed so hard that he had to sit down.

Cheetah bowed, became tangled in her paper cape, rolled once, and popped back up.

“I used my shrink ray,” she said proudly. “I wanted to see whether the world was funny from down here.”

“Is it?” Bear asked.

“Your nose is enormous through that glass.”

Bear moved the lens toward Papa.

Cheetah gasped. “Two enormous noses!”

All three laughed again.

Papa carefully placed the sheet of white paper beside Cheetah, making a smooth path away from the dust. She climbed aboard, using the button plate as a step.

Bear held the magnifying glass above her.

“We found you.”

“You found all my clues,” Cheetah replied. “Even the cheese?”

“Especially the cheese.”

The TOP SECRET device gave a cheerful ding.

A yellow message appeared across its glass front:

RECOMMENDED TINY TIME: TWENTY MINUTES

Then another line appeared:

CURRENT TINY TIME: FORTY-SEVEN MINUTES

Cheetah’s smile became slightly less enormous.

“Oh,” she said. “I may have spent too long making the button plate.”

Chapter 4 — Too Long at Tiny

Papa turned the TOP SECRET machine so its yellow message faced them.

“Forty-seven minutes,” Bear read.

“Fifty-one now,” Cheetah squeaked from the white paper.

A new line blinked beneath the timer:

AFTER TWENTY MINUTES: USE CAREFUL RETURN MODE

Bear swallowed. “What happens if we don’t?”

Papa opened a little instruction flap. The words inside were far too small to read normally, so Bear held the magnifying glass above them.

Papa read aloud. “No danger. But ordinary return mode may cause temporary size bouncing, unexpected sneezing, or spots arriving several seconds after the rest of the cheetah.”

Cheetah looked down at herself.

“I would prefer my spots to travel with me.”

“Careful return mode,” Bear said.

The machine needed three things: Cheetah’s current height, the exact time she had been tiny, and five seconds of complete stillness on its measuring square.

Bear found the square behind the machine. To him, it was the size of a postage stamp. To Cheetah, it was a dance floor.

She stepped onto it and struck a heroic pose.

The machine beeped.

PLEASE HOLD STILL

“I am still,” Cheetah said.

Her tail swished.

The machine beeped again.

TAIL IS ALSO CHEETAH

Bear laughed despite himself. Papa covered a smile with one hand.

Cheetah wrapped her tail around her feet. “Rude but accurate.”

Bear steadied the magnifying glass while Papa adjusted the height dial. The scale showed lines finer than hairs. Bear moved the dial one click at a time until its marker matched the top of Cheetah’s paper cape.

“Current height set,” he said.

Papa entered fifty-two minutes.

Cheetah held still.

One second.

Two.

At three seconds, she sneezed.

The paper cape flew over her head.

MEASUREMENT INTERRUPTED

“I have been attacked by my own costume,” she announced.

Bear carefully lifted the cape away with the corner of the white paper. “You don’t need it to become big.”

“But it is very stylish.”

“Extremely.”

They tried again.

Cheetah tucked her tail. Bear aligned the lens. Papa checked the timer.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

The machine chimed.

CAREFUL RETURN READY

A green button rose from the panel. It was tiny to Bear but almost as wide as Cheetah.

“I should push it,” she said. “It’s my shrink ray.”

Papa placed the button plate behind her like a brace. Bear held the white paper steady. Cheetah leaned both front paws against the green button.

It did not move.

She backed up three steps, ran forward, and shoved.

Click.

A soft silver light surrounded her.

Cheetah grew from thumb-sized to cracker-sized.

“Progress!” she cried.

Then to shoe-sized.

“More progress!”

Then to chair-sized.

“Please move the chair!”

Bear and Papa scooted backward as Cheetah gently expanded to her usual size.

Her paws landed on the rug.

Her tail appeared at exactly the correct time.

All her spots arrived with her.

Cheetah sprang upward in celebration and landed with a tremendous—

THWUMP!

The cracker bowl bounced. Three crackers leaped out and landed on Papa’s head.

Bear laughed. Cheetah laughed. Papa removed one cracker from each ear and laughed too.

The TOP SECRET machine displayed one final message:

RETURN COMPLETE. NEXT TIME, SET AN ALARM.

Cheetah nodded solemnly.

“Next time,” she said, “two alarms.”

Chapter 5 — Exactly the Right Size

Before leaving Cheetah’s house, Papa switched off the shrink ray. Bear placed the TOP SECRET machine in its padded box.

Cheetah added a new label:

TOP SECRET — READ THE TIMER FIRST

Then she set two alarms.

The first alarm rang immediately.

The second alarm was already ringing inside the box.

Cheetah stared at it. “Perhaps I need an alarm for setting the alarms.”

Bear laughed all the way back to Papa Bear Home.

On the deck, they shared cocoa and the crackers that had not landed on Papa’s head. Bear set the magnifying glass beside the smallest crumb.

Through the lens, it looked like a mountain.

Cheetah lowered one golden eye to the glass. Bear’s nose appeared on the other side, enormous and round.

“Still enormous,” she said.

Bear moved aside so Papa could look.

Cheetah pointed. “Also enormous.”

Papa nodded thoughtfully. “Important scientific result.”

They all laughed again.

Bear had been scared when Cheetah disappeared. He had not made the feeling go away by pretending. He and Papa had slowed down, checked what they knew, and searched carefully until the tiny clues made sense.

“I’m sorry the surprise scared you,” Cheetah said.

“I’m glad you answered the safety check,” Bear replied. “And the tiny THWUMP was very funny.”

Cheetah stood on the deck rail and prepared one final demonstration.

She jumped.

THWUMP!

The ordinary-sized sound shook one last cracker from the plate.

Papa caught it.

Soon the magnifying glass rested safely in its case. The shrink ray remained switched off. Cheetah curled beside Bear’s chair, exactly her usual size.

The deck grew quiet beneath the evening sky. Papa stayed close. Bear’s breathing softened. And at Papa Bear Home, every pawprint, spot, tail, and friend was right where it belonged.
