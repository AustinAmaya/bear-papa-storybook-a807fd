Preamble (delivered)

Bear and Papa landed on a small floating island — one lonely chunk of grass, one tree, and not much else. Skyblock survival mode. The hardest kind of Minecraft there is.

"Where do we even start?" Papa asked, looking around at the empty sky.

A portal shimmered, and out stepped JJ, red hoodie and notebook in hand. "Mikey's at the beach this week, so it's just me. Skyblock's tricky — we'll need supplies fast."

Bear grinned and quietly patted his own inventory. He'd been planning this for days.

"Actually," Bear said, "I think I can help with that."

Chapter 1 (delivered)

"Wait," JJ said, staring at Bear's open inventory. "Is that... a FULL set of tools? Wood pickaxe, wood axe, wood shovel — how do you already have all this?"

Bear grinned. "I planned ahead. I've got extra stuff too — stone, some coal, even a few saplings."

Papa blinked. "You did all this before we even landed?"

"Yep," Bear said proudly. "I figured Skyblock would be tricky, so I got ready."

JJ flipped open his notebook, already scribbling. "Okay, this changes everything. Where do we start?"

Bear looked around their little floating island — one patch of grass, one lonely tree, a small pool of water. "Same place everyone starts. Wood."

"The tree?" Papa asked. "Just the one?"

"Just the one," Bear said, walking up to it. "But that's the trick — you don't need a forest. You just need to use what you cut down wisely."

He swung his fists first, since he hadn't equipped an axe yet, breaking off the bottom log with a few solid hits. The tree cracked and dropped a full block of wood, which Bear scooped up immediately.

"Whoa, it just floats there without falling?" JJ asked, watching the tree hover oddly with its bottom missing.

"Yep," Bear said. "Minecraft trees are weird like that. Keep chopping — the wood doesn't disappear."

He kept working upward, breaking log after log, the tree slowly disappearing block by block until only leaves drifted where the trunk had been. Bear passed handfuls of wood to Papa and JJ.

"Now the important part," Bear said, holding up four planks. "You always turn some logs into crafting tables and tool handles right away. Don't just stack it in your pocket and forget it."

He demonstrated, converting logs into planks, then planks into sticks, laying out a small crafting table on the grass.

"See? Now we can make more tools whenever we need them," Bear explained. "The tree's already given us everything we need to get started."

JJ examined the crafting table with real admiration. "This is smart. Most people panic on Skyblock islands because they don't think ahead."

"That's the secret," Bear said. "Always be ready before you need it."

Papa ruffled Bear's fur, impressed. "I don't know where you learned to plan like this, but I'm glad you did."

Bear beamed. "There's more where that came from. Come on — let's see what else we can build."

Behind them, the little tree stood bare but intact, its lowest branches already beginning to sprout new leaves, patient and quiet under the endless open sky.

Chapter 2 (delivered)

With wood in hand, the real building began. Bear laid out stone blocks from his stockpile in a neat square, forming the beginnings of a proper little shelter.

"This is where your planning helps too," Bear told JJ. "You're good at knowing what we'll need before we need it."

JJ grinned, flipping to a fresh notebook page. "I like to sketch things out first. Walls here, a door there, torches every few blocks so nothing spawns inside."

"Exactly," Bear said. "Preparation and planning — that's basically the whole game."

Papa placed a block where JJ pointed, testing the fit. "You two make a good team. I feel like I'm just here to carry things."

"You're here to ask good questions," Bear said. "That's important too."

Together they built up the walls, framed a simple roof, and dropped torches along the inside edges until the whole shelter glowed warm and safe. Bear added a small dirt patch outside with the saplings from his stockpile, patting the soil down carefully.

"What's that for?" Papa asked.

"Saplings grow into trees," Bear explained. "One tree wasn't going to last us. If we plant a few, we'll never run out of wood again."

JJ nodded approvingly, jotting it down. "Smart. Long-term thinking."

With the shelter finished and a tiny sapling farm started, the three of them stood back to admire their work. The island already looked less lonely — a real little home in the endless sky.

"Okay," Bear said, "now that we're set up, we should explore a bit. See what else is out here."

They walked to the island's edge, peering out at the drifting patches of land scattered across the sky like stepping stones. Not far off floated a small rocky outcrop, mostly bare except for a patch of gravel — and something square and wooden sitting right in the middle of it.

"Is that... a chest?" Papa asked, squinting.

JJ leaned forward, notebook forgotten for a moment. "Looks like it. Skyblock maps sometimes have loot floating around like that."

Bear's eyes lit up. "A chest could have all kinds of useful stuff inside. Should we go check it out?"

"Carefully," JJ said, already sounding a little cautious. "Things that just sit out in the open like that sometimes have a catch."

Papa looked between the boys, both clearly excited despite the caution in JJ's voice. "Well, we've got sturdy shoes and clear heads. Let's go take a look — carefully, like JJ said."

They gathered a few extra blocks to bridge the gap between islands, laying them down one by one across the empty sky, until a narrow path connected their little base to the mysterious floating rock and its waiting chest.

Chapter 3 (delivered)

They crossed the last few blocks of bridge and stepped onto the rocky little island. Up close, the chest looked ordinary enough — plain wood, a simple metal latch — sitting in the middle of a patch of gravel.

Bear reached out eagerly. "Let's see what's—"

"WAIT!" JJ's voice cracked sharp with alarm, and he grabbed Bear's arm before he could touch it. "Don't open it!"

Bear froze. "What? Why?"

JJ pointed at the ground around the chest, where faint red lines crisscrossed just beneath the gravel, almost invisible unless you knew exactly what to look for. "Right there. See that red? That's redstone dust. It means this whole area is wired to something. If we open that chest, or even step in the wrong spot, it could trigger a trap."

Papa leaned in, squinting hard at the reddish lines. "I never would have noticed that."

"You have to look close," JJ said, his usual calm cracking a little with nerves. "Mikey and I found one just like this once. It nearly buried us in arrows."

Bear's excitement dimmed into careful focus. "Okay. So what do we do? We still want what's inside, right?"

"We just need to be smart about it," JJ said, flipping open his notebook with shaking hands and sketching a rough map of the redstone lines he could see. "The wiring runs from somewhere — probably a pressure plate near the chest, or hidden under it."

Bear crouched down, thinking hard. "What if we don't touch the chest at all first? What if we dig around it instead — from the side, away from where the redstone lines are — and see what's actually connected to what?"

JJ's eyes widened. "That's... actually really smart. If we can see the whole mechanism before we trigger anything, we'll know exactly what's safe to touch."

"Let's try it," Papa said. "Carefully."

Bear pulled out his stone shovel and began digging a careful trench a few blocks away from the chest, well clear of any red lines JJ had mapped. Dirt and gravel came away in small, deliberate scoops, Bear working slow and steady instead of rushing.

"There," Bear said, pointing. A shallow pressure plate sat buried just beneath the surface, directly in front of where someone would normally step to reach the chest — connected by a thin red wire running straight to a hidden dispenser tucked into the rock nearby.

JJ leaned over the hole, comparing it to his sketch. "That's it. That's the trigger. If you'd stepped up to open the chest normally, that dispenser would have fired straight at you."

Papa exhaled, relief washing over his face. "Good thing we were careful."

"Really good thing," Bear agreed, feeling a small shiver at how close it had been.

Working together now, they carefully placed blocks over the pressure plate to keep it from ever triggering, approaching the chest instead from the safe side Bear had dug clear. JJ kept watch, calling out instructions from his notebook sketch, while Bear eased the chest open at last.

Inside sat a neat stack of iron ingots, a handful of emeralds, and — best of all — a spare enchanted book, glowing faintly even in the daylight.

"YES!" Bear whooped, unable to contain himself any longer now that the danger had passed.

"Great find," JJ said, grinning as he snapped his notebook shut. "And a great catch on my part, if I do say so myself."

"The best catch," Papa said warmly, ruffling JJ's hair. "You probably just saved us a face full of arrows."

Bear scooped the loot carefully into his inventory, still buzzing with excitement. "Careful and clever. That's a good combination."

"The very best combination," JJ agreed, already sketching the whole adventure into his notebook to remember later.

They packed up, casting one last look at the now-empty, harmless chest sitting quiet on its little rock, and started the walk back across their bridge toward home, their pockets considerably heavier than before.

Chapter 4 (delivered)

Halfway back across the bridge, the sky had begun to dim, streaks of orange fading into deepening blue. Bear glanced up, suddenly uneasy.

"It's getting dark faster than I thought," he said.

"We should hurry," JJ said, quickening his pace. "Things spawn at night, and out here in the open, that's not great."

As if summoned by the words themselves, a low groan echoed from somewhere behind them. They turned to see a green, shambling shape climbing up over the edge of the rocky island they'd just left, drawn by the noise of their digging.

"Zombie!" Papa said, stepping instinctively in front of Bear.

But Bear didn't flinch. He reached into his inventory and pulled out something he'd tucked away earlier — a plain wooden sword, one of the very first tools he'd prepared before they'd even landed.

"I've got this," Bear said, gripping the handle firm.

"Bear, are you sure—" Papa started.

"I planned for this too," Bear said, already moving toward the bridge's edge where the zombie was climbing up, awkward and slow. "Wooden swords aren't much, but they work if you're careful."

The zombie lurched forward with a groan, arms outstretched. Bear stepped back just out of reach, then darted in with a quick swing, striking its shoulder before hopping back again.

"Hit and retreat," Bear called over his shoulder. "Don't just stand there and trade hits — you'll take way more damage that way."

JJ grabbed a loose stone block and lobbed it to distract the zombie's attention, buying Bear another opening. Papa hovered close, ready to jump in but trusting Bear to lead.

Bear darted in again, sword flashing, landing another solid hit. The zombie staggered, momentarily thrown off balance on the narrow bridge.

"One more should do it!" JJ shouted.

Bear took a breath, timed his last strike carefully, and swung true. The zombie let out a final groan and crumbled away into nothing, leaving behind a small scatter of rotten flesh on the bridge.

For a moment, nobody moved. Then Bear let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding, lowering his sword.

"You did it!" Papa said, relief and pride tangled together in his voice. He knelt down to Bear's level. "That was really brave. And smart — hit and retreat, just like you said."

"I read about it," Bear admitted, still a little breathless. "I wanted to be ready, just in case."

JJ clapped him on the shoulder. "Well, it worked. First zombie defeated, no scratches. Not bad for someone using a wooden sword."

"Let's get back before another one shows up," Papa said, glancing warily at the darkening sky.

They hurried the rest of the way across the bridge, torches from their little base glowing warm and welcoming in the distance, guiding them home through the settling dark.

Chapter 5 (delivered)

They made it back to the little island just as full night settled over the sky, stars scattering bright above the tiny patch of grass they now called home. The shelter's torches glowed warm through the doorway, a small golden square of safety against the dark.

Inside, they set their new loot on the floor — the iron, the emeralds, the glowing enchanted book — and Papa let out a long, happy breath.

"What a day," he said, sinking down against the wall. "Wood, walls, a trapped chest, and a zombie, all before dinner."

JJ laughed, flipping to a fresh page in his notebook to sketch the whole adventure. "I've explored plenty of Skyblock worlds with Mikey, but I don't think I've ever had a partner this prepared before."

"High praise," Bear said, grinning as he settled onto the floor beside them.

Papa reached over and ruffled Bear's fur. "You really did think of everything today. The tools, the stockpile, even the sword when we needed it most."

"I just didn't want us to get stuck," Bear said. "Figured it was better to be ready than sorry."

"That's a good way to live," Papa said softly. "Being ready doesn't mean you're scared of what's coming. It just means you can meet it calmly, whatever it is."

JJ nodded, closing his notebook at last. "Careful planning and clear eyes. That's basically the whole secret to surviving out here."

Outside, the sapling farm sat quiet under the stars, tiny green shoots already reaching upward, promising more wood for tomorrow. The island, once so bare and lonely, now felt like a real home — small, but full.

Bear yawned, the day's excitement finally catching up with him. "Tell Mikey he missed a good one."

"Oh, he's going to be so jealous," JJ said, chuckling.

Papa pulled soft wool blocks together into something like a bed, and the three of them settled in for the night, torches keeping watch outside while they rested.

"Best Skyblock day ever," Bear mumbled, already half asleep.

"Best Skyblock day ever," Papa agreed, closing his eyes as the little island drifted quiet and safe under the endless, star-filled sky.
