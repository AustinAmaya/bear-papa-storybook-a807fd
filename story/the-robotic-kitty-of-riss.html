Preamble

At Papa Bear Home, Bear and Aurora—the bright pink kitty—were building a tower of magnetic blocks when Papa’s communicator beeped.

Captain Bolts appeared on its tiny screen. “RISS needs three gentle helpers. A new robotic kitty arrived today, and zero gravity is making her first shift rather... bouncy.”

Behind him, something silver zipped past. Two green eyes flashed. A metal tail made a cheerful sproing.

Aurora’s ears perked up. “A robotic kitty?”

Bear grinned. “On a robot space station?”

Papa picked up the space bag. “Then we remember the Floaty Rules: tiny pushes, gentle grabs, and think before zooming.”

A silver shuttle clicked onto the deck. Its door opened toward the stars.

What should the robotic kitty be doing when they arrive, and what should they investigate afterward?

A. Magnetic Yarn — she is tangled in floating magnetic yarn; after helping her, they follow glowing pawprints toward the observation dome.

B. The Wobbly Toolbox — she is delivering a toolbox whose compartments keep opening; after securing it, they investigate tiny tapping sounds behind a station wall.

C. Zero-Gravity Jumps — she is practicing jumps and bouncing between handles; after slowing down, they follow a blinking signal from her tail toward the hydroponic garden.

Chapter 1 — The Magnetic Yarn Tangle

The silver shuttle docked with RISS using three gentle clicks.

Inside the airlock, Captain Bolts waited beside Wrenchley and Beep. Beep gave an excited beep-beep!

“Welcome back,” said Captain Bolts. “Our new robotic kitty is somewhere behind that yarn.”

A fuzzy ball of blue magnetic yarn floated past. Two green eyes blinked from its middle.

“Hello,” said a muffled voice. “I am Mittens-Seven. My organizing demonstration has become an un-organizing demonstration.”

Aurora tried not to giggle. “Do you prefer Mittens-Seven or Mittens?”

“Mittens, please.”

One strand clung to Mittens’s metal tail. Another loop circled her silver body. Every time she wiggled, the magnetic yarn pulled tighter against her metal panels.

Bear reached toward the nearest loop, then stopped. “If we tug one piece, the magnets might pull the others with it.”

Papa nodded. “First stop the motion. Then find where the tangle begins.”

Aurora caught the loose spool with a soft station strap. Papa held a blue handrail and steadied the yarn ball. Bear followed one strand with his eyes until he found the outermost loop.

“Beep, can you count each loop?” Bear asked.

“Beep!”

Bear lifted the first loop over Mittens’s tail.

“Beep.”

Aurora guided the second loop past one silver ear.

“Beep.”

Papa opened the third loop instead of pulling it tight.

“Beep.”

Slowly, the yarn became one tidy floating coil. Mittens stretched all four legs and made a pleased mechanical purr-rrrr.

“My movement systems are free,” she said. “Thank you.”

Captain Bolts showed them Mittens’s three helper tools: magnetic paw pads, whisker airflow sensors, and a tiny picture projector in her tail.

Before anyone could test them, the end of Mittens’s tail began blinking green, green, purple.

She tilted her head. “That is a station alert. It is coming from the hydroponic garden.”

The garden visit was already their next step. If the mystery became harder afterward, one of Mittens’s tools could become especially useful in Chapter 3.

Which tool should they prepare to use later?

A. Her magnetic paw pads.

B. Her whisker airflow sensors.

C. Her tail picture projector.

Chapter 2 — The Blinking Garden

Mittens’s tail blinked green, green, purple as she led them through RISS.

Bear moved with tiny pushes along the blue handrails. Aurora followed, her pink fur glowing softly beneath the station lights. Papa came last, keeping everyone close without crowding them.

At the hydroponic garden door, Mittens’s blinking sped up.

“Alert source confirmed,” she said.

The door slid open.

Rows of lettuce, tomatoes, and curly star-vines grew from wall trays. Silver lamps shone overhead. But dozens of water droplets floated through the room like clear marbles.

One bounced gently off Aurora’s nose.

She blinked. “The garden sneezed on me.”

A small garden screen flashed:

WATER FLOW: WOBBLY LIGHT CYCLE: UNCERTAIN

Papa pointed to a drip tube that had popped loose from a root tray. Each pulse sent another round drop into the air.

“First, stop making more droplets,” Bear said.

Wrenchley closed the garden valve. Bear and Aurora used a plain soft mesh scoop to collect the floating water while Papa reconnected the tube. Mittens held the storage bottle steady without activating any of her special tools.

“Why does the water make balls?” Aurora asked.

“Surface tension,” Papa said. “Water molecules pull toward one another. On Earth, gravity flattens and drops the water. Up here, that inward pull makes floating spheres.”

Bear guided the last sphere into the bottle. “Good for looking at. Bad for control panels.”

“Correct,” said Mittens.

The water warning disappeared, but the light warning remained.

Bear looked around. The left grow lamp flashed green. The right lamp flashed green. Then both flashed purple.

Green, green, purple.

“The same pattern as Mittens’s tail,” Bear said.

Mittens checked the screen. “My tail is repeating the garden controller’s help signal. I am the messenger, not the cause.”

Below the lamps, the star-vines leaned left, then right, as the lights switched too quickly. Their leaves could not settle toward either lamp.

Papa opened the controller’s inspection cover. Behind a clear panel, a tiny silver timing bead floated loose beside the turning light wheel. It was supposed to sit in a round holder and mark one slow day-and-night cycle.

“There’s the real problem,” Bear said. “The timer cannot tell when one light cycle ends.”

The bead was sealed safely inside the controller. Fixing it would require one of Mittens’s special tools—the tool Bear would choose after Chapter 1. Until then, they left the cover closed and the lamps on a safe steady setting.

Once the main garden repair was finished, one smaller station task could shape Chapter 4.

Which later task should they handle?

A. Guide a floating tray of seedlings safely away from an air vent.

B. Find Mittens’s missing charging cushion by following its soft purr signal.

C. Deliver the garden’s first ripe space strawberry to Captain Bolts without letting it float away.

Chapter 3 — The Timing Bead

Papa kept the grow lamps on their safe steady setting while Wrenchley brought the controller manual.

“The timing bead is magnetic,” Papa said. “That is why it stays in its round holder during zero gravity.”

“And why Mittens can reach it,” Bear said.

Mittens raised one silver paw. The pad beneath it glowed blue. A loose washer drifted from Wrenchley’s tool belt and clicked onto her paw.

“Magnetic paw pads ready,” she announced.

Wrenchley switched off the controller wheel. It slowed, but the silver timing bead did not drift free. It snapped against the wheel’s metal axle instead.

Mittens placed her back paws on the marked anchor plate beside the controller. Click. Click. She was securely attached.

Papa opened the small service port. “Only the controller is off. The garden’s safe steady lights remain on.”

Mittens reached one front paw through the port.

The timing bead trembled. Then it slid toward her.

“Good,” Bear whispered.

But the bead stopped halfway. The axle pulled one way. Mittens’s paw pulled the other.

Mittens increased her magnet strength.

The bead jerked forward—and the metal service ring pulled Mittens’s paw sideways with a loud CLANK!

Her body twisted. One back paw popped loose from the anchor plate.

Aurora caught the station strap attached to Mittens’s harness. Bear grabbed a blue handrail. Papa planted both feet beneath a floor loop and steadied Aurora.

Nobody flew away, but Mittens’s green eyes flashed quickly.

“I have made the problem more magnetic,” she said.

Papa breathed in slowly.

Bear and Aurora matched him.

Papa breathed out. “We are anchored. The lights are safe. We have time to think.”

Bear looked through the clear panel. Pulling harder had made Mittens stick to the wrong metal. The bead was still pressed against the axle.

“We need her magnet to pull only the bead,” Bear said. “But magnets pull other magnetic things too.”

Wrenchley held up a thin clear plastic guide. “Nonmagnetic.”

Bear’s eyes brightened. “We can put that between the bead and the axle. Then Mittens will not need a stronger pull. She only needs the bead a little closer to her paw.”

Papa nodded. “Magnetic force becomes stronger at shorter distances. Small distance, small movement.”

Aurora kept the harness strap steady while Mittens lowered her magnet strength. Her paw released from the service ring.

Wrenchley slid the plastic guide through a second port. Bear watched from the clear panel and directed him.

“Left a little. Down. Hold steady.”

The guide slipped between the timing bead and the axle.

The bead floated free.

Mittens brought one paw close—not touching, not rushing. The bead crossed the small gap and landed softly against her glowing pad.

Tick.

“Captured,” she said.

Now they had to place it in the round holder without attracting the holder’s metal rim. Bear pointed to a narrow plastic groove leading straight into the center.

“Slide it along the groove,” he said. “The plastic keeps your paw away from the rim.”

Mittens moved one careful inch at a time. Aurora counted each movement.

“One. Two. Three.”

Mittens lowered her magnet strength.

The bead settled into its holder.

Click.

Wrenchley closed the service ports. Papa restarted the controller.

The left grow lamp brightened slowly. Later, it dimmed as the right lamp rose. No flashing. No confused switching.

The star-vines turned their leaves toward the light. One curled bud opened into a small golden flower.

Mittens’s tail stopped blinking and gave one calm green glow.

“Garden cycle stable,” she said. Then she looked at Bear. “Your plan used less power.”

“And more thinking,” Aurora added.

Captain Bolts’s voice came through the garden speaker. “RISS records this repair as: do not solve every magnetic problem with more magnet.”

Beep gave an approving beep!

The main garden mystery was solved. After the smaller station task chosen from Chapter 2, it would be time for a gentle goodbye in Chapter 5.

How should they finish their visit?

A. Rest by the Earth window while Mittens projects a quiet field of stars.

B. Wind the rescued magnetic yarn into friendship bracelets and share space snacks.

C. Make Mittens a cozy charging spot beside Aurora, then exchange blinking goodnight signals at the airlock.

Chapter 4 — The First Space Strawberry

The repaired grow lamps moved through one slow, steady cycle. Under their gentle light, a strawberry near the center tray changed from pale green to bright red.

Wrenchley checked the garden screen. “First ripe fruit of the new cycle.”

Mittens’s green eyes widened. “Captain Bolts should receive it.”

Aurora floated closer. “How do we deliver a strawberry in zero gravity without turning it into a runaway snack?”

Bear studied the berry. If they clipped its stem normally, it would float wherever the smallest push sent it.

“We catch it before we cut it,” he said.

Wrenchley brought a clear snack capsule with a soft lid. Aurora threaded a safety tether through its handle. Mittens attached one magnetic paw to the metal garden rail and held the capsule open with the other.

Papa checked their plan. “Capsule under the berry. Tether clipped. Lid ready. Then cut.”

Bear guided the capsule around the strawberry without squeezing it. Aurora clipped the tether to her belt loop. Papa used small garden scissors on the stem.

Snip.

The strawberry drifted down less than an inch and landed inside the capsule.

Mittens closed the lid.

Click.

“Runaway snack prevented,” she said.

They started toward the command module. Mittens carried the capsule on a little magnetic tray while Bear and Aurora moved along the blue handrails. Papa followed with the garden report.

Halfway through a round connecting tunnel, the walls changed from steel to a lightweight nonmagnetic material.

Mittens’s tray gave a tiny tick.

Then it lifted away from her paw.

“The tray is not attached!” Aurora said.

The clear capsule began drifting toward the middle of the tunnel—but the safety tether stayed clipped to Aurora.

Papa held one palm flat. “No lunging. The strawberry is contained, and the tether is holding.”

Aurora stopped herself at the nearest handle. Bear caught the tether gently between both hands. Mittens anchored her back paws to a metal floor strip and reached forward.

“Tiny pull,” Bear said.

Aurora drew in one short length of tether. Bear passed it to Mittens. Then another. The capsule returned slowly, like a very small fish on a very careful line.

Mittens tucked it against her chest. “For nonmagnetic tunnels, I will use the strap.”

“Good tools still need the right place,” Bear said.

At the command module, Captain Bolts waited beside Beep. Beep’s lights blinked with excitement.

Bear held the capsule while Aurora opened the lid. Mittens guided the strawberry into Captain Bolts’s waiting snack bowl.

“Delivery complete,” Bear announced. “One strawberry. Zero escapes.”

Captain Bolts examined the bright red fruit. “RISS records this as its first successful garden harvest after the Great Light Wobble.”

“The wobble lasted eleven minutes,” Wrenchley said.

“History can still have a good name,” Captain Bolts replied.

He sliced the strawberry into small pieces for Bear, Papa, and Aurora, then placed one tiny slice on Mittens’s sensor plate so she could analyze its smell and color.

Mittens gave a delighted mechanical purr. “Result: excellent strawberry.”

The garden was steady. The delivery was complete. With the square Earth glowing beyond the window, everyone headed toward the common room for friendship bracelets, space snacks, and one cozy final project before home.

Chapter 5 — Bracelets and Blinking Goodnights

Captain Bolts returned the coil of blue magnetic yarn. Wrenchley switched it to its gentle craft setting, and Beep passed out little silver clasps with cheerful beeps.

Bear wound a bracelet around Papa’s hand, then made one for himself. Aurora shaped hers into two neat pink-and-blue loops. Mittens held the yarn steady with her magnetic paws—but only on the metal clasp.

“Correct tool. Correct place,” she said.

They shared crunchy star crackers and strawberry-flavored space snacks while Earth drifted beyond the window, blue and quiet beneath them.

Then Bear opened the space bag. Inside was a folded silver blanket.

“For your charging spot,” he told Mittens.

Papa secured one corner to the common-room wall. Aurora tucked the blanket into a soft floating nest. Bear used the remaining magnetic yarn to make a tidy blue edge, and Wrenchley connected a charging lead beneath it.

Mittens curled into the center beside Aurora. Her green eyes softened to two sleepy lines.

“Charging spot comfort level: excellent,” she murmured.

Aurora rested one paw beside Mittens’s silver paw. “Kitty approved?”

A warm mechanical purr answered her.

Soon it was time to go. At the airlock, Mittens blinked green, green, purple. Bear answered by tapping his bracelet twice, then once.

Captain Bolts saluted. Wrenchley waved. Beep gave one soft beep.

The shuttle carried Bear, Papa, and Aurora back through the dark, starry quiet. Papa knelt beside Bear when they landed on the deck.

“You solved a big problem without using a bigger force,” Papa said.

Bear looked at his bracelet. “We slowed down enough to see the smaller answer.”

Little Polar Bear watched from his neighborhood post. Little Red Bear rested warmly on the deck as they stepped inside Papa Bear Home.

Aurora curled up nearby. Bear settled beneath his blanket. Papa stayed close while the room became still.

Far above, a robotic kitty charged in her silver nest, wearing a blue bracelet of her own.

Everything was fixed. Everyone was safe. And bedtime had landed softly at home.
