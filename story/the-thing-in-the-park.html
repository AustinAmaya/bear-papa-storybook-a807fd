The Thing in the Park

Bear and Papa discover that the little thing watching them in the park is only a shy squirrel hoping to play.

Chapter 1 — The Best Slide Day

The park was almost empty, which meant Bear could run as fast as his sneakers wanted.

“Papa! Watch this!” he called.

Papa looked up from tying the picnic blanket to a bench leg so the wind would not steal it. “I am watching with both eyes and one eyebrow.”

Bear climbed the twisty ladder, sat at the top of the blue slide, and zoomed down so fast his fur ruffled backward.

“Again!” Bear shouted.

“An important scientific result,” Papa said. “The slide still works.”

They did swings next. Papa pushed gently, then Bear pumped his legs by himself.

Higher.

Higher.

Not too high. Just perfect high.

The trees around the park whispered in the soft evening breeze.

And from one leafy branch, something small watched.

It did not growl. It did not pounce. It only blinked two round eyes and hugged the tree trunk like it was trying to be brave.

Bear did not see it.

Papa did not see it.

Not yet.

Chapter 2 — The Wobbly Picnic

After the swings, Bear and Papa opened their snack box.

Apple slices. Crackers. Two little cheese squares. One water bottle for Bear and one for Papa.

Papa held up a cracker. “To the park.”

Bear tapped his cracker against Papa’s. “To the slide.”

Just then, a tiny acorn rolled across the picnic blanket.

Bonk.

It bumped Bear’s shoe.

Bear looked down. “Hello, acorn.”

Another acorn rolled after it.

Bonk.

Then a third.

Bonk.

Papa narrowed his eyes in his thinking way. “Either the tree is sharing, or someone upstairs is doing bowling.”

Bear looked up into the branches.

Leaves wiggled.

A small brown tail vanished behind them.

“Papa,” Bear whispered, delighted. “Something is watching us.”

Papa scooted closer. “Then let’s be kind watchers back.”

Chapter 3 — The Brave Little Squirrel

Bear stayed very still.

Not statue still. Kid-who-is-trying-really-hard still.

Papa put one cracker crumb on the edge of the blanket. Then he moved his hand back.

“We do not grab park friends,” Papa said softly.

“We invite,” Bear whispered.

The leaves trembled.

A tiny squirrel peeked out.

It had bright black eyes, a fluffy tail, and one crumb stuck to its whisker from some earlier squirrel business.

Bear smiled with his whole face.

The squirrel came down one branch. Then another.

Then it froze.

A big yellow ball rolled out from behind the bushes and bumped the tree.

Thump.

The squirrel squeaked and zipped halfway back up.

Bear stood slowly. “It’s okay. It’s just a ball.”

Papa picked up the ball. “A runaway ball. Very suspicious. Probably from the soccer field.”

Bear held out his hands. “We can move it away.”

Together, they rolled the ball gently back toward the grass.

The squirrel watched.

This time, Bear knew it was watching.

And it did not feel scary anymore.

Chapter 4 — The Game Everyone Could Play

The squirrel came down to the bottom branch.

Bear sat cross-legged on the blanket. Papa sat beside him.

The squirrel sniffed the cracker crumb.

Then it picked it up in its tiny paws and nibbled.

Crunch crunch crunch.

Bear whispered, “It likes picnic.”

Papa whispered back, “Many great friendships begin with snacks.”

The squirrel flicked its tail.

Then it dropped an acorn.

Bonk.

It rolled straight to Bear.

Bear laughed. “Is this a game?”

He rolled the acorn gently back toward the tree.

The squirrel caught it with both paws.

Then it dropped it again.

Bonk.

Papa gasped. “Acorn bowling. I knew it.”

They played three rounds. Bear rolled carefully. Papa kept score in the air with his finger. The squirrel won every time, because squirrels are excellent at squirrel games.

Chapter 5 — Home Before Dark

The sky turned peachy pink.

Papa checked the time. “One more acorn roll, then home.”

Bear nodded. He rolled the acorn softly.

The squirrel caught it, tucked it under one paw, and gave a tiny chirp.

“Goodbye,” Bear said. “Thanks for watching us.”

Papa folded the blanket. “Maybe it was shy.”

“Maybe it wanted to play,” Bear said.

“Maybe both.”

They walked home as the first stars came out. Bear held Papa’s hand. The park grew quiet behind them, but not lonely. Up in the tree, the little squirrel watched them go, safe in its leaves.

At Papa Bear Home, Bear washed his hands and climbed into bed.

Papa tucked the blanket around him.

“Sometimes something watching is just someone wondering if it is safe to say hello,” Papa said.

Bear’s eyes got sleepy.

“Next time,” he murmured, “we can bring extra crackers.”

Papa smiled. “Next park day.”

Outside, the night settled soft and still.

Goodnight, Bear.
