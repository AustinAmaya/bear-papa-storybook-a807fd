Pikachu in the Backyard

Bear teaches Papa Pikachu's favorite tricks, and together they learn to breathe through a big, sparkly moment.

Preamble (delivered)

Bear and Papa were out in the backyard at Papa Bear Home, enjoying a warm, sunny afternoon. Bear had a surprise.

"Papa, come quick!" Bear whispered. "Look in the tall grass!"

Papa knelt down, squinting. Two bright eyes peeked out at him. Then a small yellow creature stepped into the light, long ears twitching, red cheeks sparking just a little.

"Whoa," Papa breathed. "What is that?"

"That's Pikachu," Bear said, grinning. "He's my friend. He's a little shy at first, so we have to be patient."

Pikachu's lightning-bolt tail swished through the air.

"Patient," Papa said, settling down cross-legged on the grass. "I can do that."

Lesson

Staying calm and breathing slowly helps you through big feelings and hard moments.

Chapter 1 (delivered)

"Okay, Papa, first lesson," Bear said, bouncing on his toes. "Pikachu knows lots of fun tricks, and I know all of them."

Pikachu peeked out from behind the garden hose, one ear still tipped down, and chirped a small, careful "Pika?"

"That means he's listening," Bear whispered. "Watch this."

Bear sat down in the grass and held out his hand, palm up. Pikachu hopped over, sniffed it, and then gave a tiny spark-hop that landed him right on Bear's knee.

"Whoa," Papa said softly. "He's so quick."

"That's his spark-hop," Bear said. "He does it when he feels brave. Your turn, Papa. Hold out your hand, nice and slow."

Papa held out his hand, very still. Pikachu looked at it for a long moment, his red cheeks crackling with a few sparks. Then he hopped over, bumped his head gently against Papa's fingers, and sat down.

"He likes you," Bear said proudly.

"I think he likes patient hands," Papa said, grinning.

Next came the tail trick. Bear showed Papa how Pikachu could balance his lightning-bolt tail on the tip of his finger, wobbling and wiggling but never falling over.

"Okay, that's harder than it looks," Papa said, after his first try made the tail flop sideways.

"Go slower," Bear said. "Hold your finger still and let the tail find its balance. That's how Pikachu does it."

Papa tried again, moving his hand so gently that the tail stayed up. Pikachu blinked, pleased, and let out a happy little "Pika-pi!"

"You got it!" Bear shouted.

Pikachu giggled, a happy chirpy sound, and then did the tickle-cheek trick. He nuzzled his round cheek against Bear's arm, and a few fizzy sparks tickled Bear's fur so that he squealed and rolled over in the grass.

"Hey, no fair!" Bear laughed.

Papa laughed too, and before long the three of them were sitting together in the sunshine, Pikachu's tail swishing happily back and forth.

"Okay," Bear said, scrambling up. "There's one more trick. But it's a secret until we get to the hill."

Pikachu's ears perked straight up. He bounced in a circle and pointed one paw toward the big sunny hill behind the house, his tail glowing faintly with excitement.

"Pika-chu!" he chirped, as if to say, Come on!

Bear took Papa's hand. Papa, still grinning, let Pikachu lead the way.

Chapter 2 (delivered)

The big sunny hill rose gently behind the house, its grass tall and golden in the afternoon light. Pikachu bounded up the slope, his tail bobbing like a lantern, and turned back to wave.

"Pika! Pika-chu!" he called.

"He wants us to hurry," Bear said, laughing.

Papa climbed slowly, huffing a little. "Okay, I'm getting a workout here."

"That's good," Bear said. "Remember, Papa, you go at your own pace. No need to rush."

At the top, the hill opened into a soft, flat meadow. Dandelion puffs floated lazily through the air. Pikachu hopped in a happy circle, then stopped and looked down at a patch of tall grass near the bottom of the far slope.

"Uh-oh," Bear said. "That's the tricky part."

The grass was thick and tangled, hiding little dips and roots underfoot. Papa squinted at it. "Is it safe to cross?"

Pikachu stood very still, his ears twitching. Then he made a soft, playful sound and tapped one paw on the ground, twice, slow and steady.

"That's his playful spark signal," Bear said. "He does it when the ground is okay to walk on. Watch his paw, Papa. Two taps means go, slow and steady."

Papa watched carefully. Pikachu tapped once more, his tail glowing faintly. Papa took a step, then another, matching Pikachu's slow rhythm.

"Like this?" Papa asked.

"Just like that," Bear said, hopping in right behind him.

Halfway across, Papa's foot slipped on a loose root. He wobbled, arms out. Pikachu chirped quickly, and Bear grabbed Papa's sleeve.

"Stop, breathe, and find your balance," Bear said. "Then keep going."

Papa stopped. He took one slow breath in, one slow breath out, and felt his feet settle firmly into the soft ground.

"Good," Papa said. "That helped."

Pikachu gave a happy "Pika-pi!" and hopped ahead to show them a clear path.

They crossed the tall grass together, step by careful step, until they reached the far side where the grass gave way to a wide, flat spot of sunshine. Pikachu sat down in the middle of it with a satisfied sigh, his tail curling around his feet.

"Okay," Bear said, catching his breath. "Now the best part. Papa, do you feel your heart going fast?"

Papa nodded, laughing. "Just a little."

"Pikachu's cheeks crackle when he gets excited," Bear said. "So you have to watch his sparks, and be ready."

Pikachu looked up at them both, and a tiny spark flickered across his cheek.

"Something big is about to happen," Bear whispered, and Papa's eyes widened.

Chapter 3 (delivered)

Papa and Bear had just caught their breath when the meadow began to hum. Not a sound you could hear with your ears, exactly, but a sound you could feel in your toes. The grass tickled their ankles. The air smelled like rain, even though the sky was clear and blue.

Then a rumble rolled across the sky, low and loud, like far-off thunder. Pikachu's ears shot straight up. Out of nowhere, a burst of bright sparks flew from his cheeks and scattered across the hilltop like a firework.

"WHOA!" Papa yelled, throwing his arms up to shield his face.

The dandelion puffs whirled in a sudden gust. The sparks crackled and popped in the air above them, glittering yellow and white. Pikachu leaped straight up, tail flaring wide, his whole body buzzing with energy.

"Pika! Pika-PI!" he cried, and the sound came out high and frantic.

Bear stumbled back a step. His heart pounded so fast that his ears went hot, and his arms felt wobbly and far away. The meadow was spinning. There was too much noise, too much light, too much everything at once.

"Bear?" Papa said, his voice tight with worry. He dropped to one knee beside him. "Are you okay?"

Bear shook his head. "It's too big. Everything's too loud."

Pikachu landed in the grass with a thump, and his sparks fizzled out. He stood trembling, eyes wide and scared, his cheeks still crackling in little bursts. Then he looked at Bear, and his ears drooped.

Papa saw both of them shaking, and he didn't panic. He took a slow, steady breath in through his nose, and let it out through his mouth, very slowly.

"Okay," Papa said quietly. "Let's breathe. Just like this. In, two, three. Out, two, three."

Bear looked at Papa, and his chest began to loosen a little. He copied the breathing, in and out, in and out.

Pikachu tilted his head, watching them both. Bear crouched low in the grass, folded his legs, and sat down.

"Pikachu," Bear said softly, "I know a trick. Come sit with me. We'll breathe together."

Pikachu hesitated, then slowly hopped over and sat beside Bear. Bear raised both hands, palms together, and lowered them slowly until they rested in front of his chest.

"That's a Zen pose," Bear whispered. "It helps you feel calm when everything feels big. Breathe in with me."

Bear breathed in through his nose. Pikachu copied him, his cheeks still crackling but not quite as fast.

"Now breathe out, slow and long."

Bear breathed out. Pikachu followed, his shoulders dropping, his tail settling down into the grass. A tiny spark popped, then another, and then they stopped altogether.

Papa sat down too, crossing his legs and placing his hands on his knees. "Count with me," he said. "One, two, three, four, in. Four, three, two, one, out."

They breathed together in the warm sunshine, the three of them, while the thunder sound faded away into a soft, gentle wind. A bird sang somewhere down the hill. The dandelion puffs drifted slowly down, settling on Pikachu's ears and whiskers like tiny white snowflakes.

Bear opened his eyes. The meadow was quiet and calm again, and the sparks on Pikachu's cheeks were tiny and sleepy now.

"Better?" Papa asked.

Pikachu nodded and gave a soft, tired "Pika."

"Better," Bear said, smiling. "Calm breathing works, even when things get big."

Papa ruffled Bear's hair gently. "You taught him that. And you taught me too. I'd forgotten how much better I feel when I slow down."

Pikachu shook a dandelion puff off his nose and sneezed a tiny, happy spark. He hopped in a small circle, then sat right back down, a little more settled than before.

"Okay, Pikachu," Bear said. "Want to try one more Zen pose? This time, let's do it with your tail."

Pikachu's tail curled up, then lowered in a slow, graceful arc, and he let out a contented breath. Bear laughed, and Papa laughed too, and the sound rolled out across the sunny hilltop, warm and easy.

Pikachu leaned against Bear's side, his tail curled around both of them, and let out a long, happy breath.

Chapter 4 (delivered)

The sun had slipped low by the time they started back down the hill. Their shadows stretched long across the grass, and the air had turned cool and golden. Bear walked beside Papa, humming a little, his hands loose at his sides and his breathing slow and easy.

"Feel better?" Papa asked.

"Yeah," Bear said. "Breathing is like a secret button. Push it, and everything gets smaller again."

Papa smiled. "I'll remember that."

They reached the bottom of the hill, crossed the tall grass with care, and wandered back toward the house. The screen door creaked as they stepped inside, and the kitchen smelled warm and familiar.

"Pikachu?" Bear called. "Ready for a snack?"

No answer.

Bear looked around the living room. No yellow ears. No swishing lightning-bolt tail. Just the couch, the bookshelf, and a soft patch of sunlight on the rug.

"Pikachu?" Bear called again, a little louder.

Papa checked behind the couch, then under the kitchen table. Nothing. He straightened up and frowned. "I thought he was right behind us."

"He was," Bear said. "When we crossed the hill, he was right there." His voice tightened, and he felt that fast, buzzy feeling begin to creep up toward his ears again.

Papa noticed. He crouched down to Bear's level and put a hand on his shoulder. "Hey. Remember what we just did on the hill?"

Bear nodded slowly.

"Let's do it again. Breathe in, two, three. Breathe out, two, three."

They breathed together, the same slow rhythm as before. Bear felt the fast, buzzy feeling loosen, and his thoughts came back into focus.

"Okay," Bear said. "Let's think. Where would Pikachu hide if he wanted to be cozy?"

Papa tapped his chin. "Somewhere warm. Somewhere quiet. Somewhere he feels safe."

They checked the laundry basket, the closet with the soft blanket, and the window seat by the kitchen. No Pikachu.

Then Bear remembered something. "Papa, Pikachu likes warm spots. Remember the sunny patch on the rug?"

They went back to the living room. The sunlight had moved, and a sleepy, rounded shape sat tucked under the blanket on the armchair, one long ear poking out.

Bear tiptoed closer and peeked. Pikachu lay curled up, eyes half closed, his lightning-bolt tail draped over his nose.

"Found you," Bear whispered, grinning.

Pikachu opened one eye, blinked, and gave a soft, sleepy "Pika."

"You were hiding in the cozy spot," Papa said, laughing quietly. "Smart choice."

Pikachu wiggled out from under the blanket, stretched his arms wide, and gave a big yawn. Then he stood up on his tiptoes, and with one small paw he tapped Bear's nose, then Papa's, as if to say, You found me!

"Good breathing, Bear," Papa said. "You didn't panic. You thought it through."

"It's easier when we do it together," Bear said. "Calm first. Then looking."

Papa nodded, a warm smile on his face. "That's a good way to handle a lot of things."

Pikachu hopped up onto the arm of the chair and sat facing them, his tail swishing lazily.

"Do you want to rest here for a bit?" Bear asked.

Pikachu nodded and yawned again.

Chapter 5 (delivered)

The evening settled over Papa Bear Home, soft and blue. Lamps glowed warm in the windows, and the crickets outside started their gentle song.

Pikachu curled up on the armchair, his tail tucked over his nose, his breathing slow and even. Every so often, a tiny spark popped on his cheek, then faded, like a sleepy firefly.

Bear sat on the rug beside him, his legs crossed, his hands resting on his knees. Papa sat on the couch, a blanket over his lap, watching them both.

"He's really tired," Bear whispered.

"Good tired," Papa said softly. "The fun kind."

Bear nodded. He thought about the hilltop, the sparks, the dandelion puffs settling on Pikachu's ears. He thought about the warm rug, the blanket, and the soft voice that had helped them both breathe.

"Papa?" he asked. "Did you have fun today?"

"The most fun," Papa said. "And I learned some tricks too. Patient hands. Slow tails. And breathing when things get big."

Bear smiled sleepily. "That's the best kind of learning."

Pikachu gave a small, contented "Pika" and shifted deeper into the blanket. His tail gave one last, lazy swish, then settled.

Papa leaned over and tucked the blanket up under Bear's chin. "Time to rest, Bear. You did a good job today."

"You did a good job too," Bear mumbled, his eyes already closing.

Outside, the sky faded from gold to deep blue. Somewhere far off, a soft breeze moved through the tall grass on the hill, and the last dandelion puff drifted gently down into the dark.

Pikachu was asleep. Bear was almost asleep. And Papa sat quietly beside them both, breathing slow and easy, as the house grew still and safe for the night.
