One Chunk of Sky

Bear and Papa join Minecraft friends JJ and Mikey to survive on a single floating Skyblock island.

Preamble (draft)

Tonight the portal behind Redbird's counter sparkled bright, and Bear and Papa stepped through into something brand new. Not solid ground under their boots — just one floating island, high in the blue Minecraft sky. Below: nothing but clouds. Above: nothing but stars. This was Skyblock — survive on a single chunk of land, with the whole wide sky all around.

Waiting on the grassy patch to greet them were two friends: JJ, careful and clever in his red hoodie, and Mikey, bouncy and curious in his turtle-green shell. Four builders. One tiny island. A whole sky waiting to be filled.

Chapter 1 (405 words)

Bear stood at the edge of the grass and looked down. Nothing below his boots but blue, all the way down, and more blue after that. No ground, no hills — just sky everywhere, like the whole world had been erased except one small square of dirt, floating alone.

"Whoa," Bear whispered, grabbing Papa's hand without thinking.

Papa looked down too, and his stomach did a little flip. "That's a lot of sky," he said carefully.

Beside them, JJ pulled his red hood tighter and stepped back from the edge. "I don't love this," JJ said. "There's no bottom."

Only Mikey wasn't worried. He walked right up to the edge, peered over with his round turtle eyes, and grinned.

"This is Skyblock!" Mikey said happily. "One little island, one little tree, that's it — that's everything we get. No shortcuts. Just us and this one chunk."

"That doesn't sound like it's supposed to make me feel better," said JJ.

Mikey counted on his fingers. "One tree, see it? One patch of dirt. A little water in that bucket. That's the whole game. Everything we build, we build from exactly what we've got."

Bear's grip loosened. He looked at the lone tree in the middle of their tiny island, leaves rustling in wind from every direction at once. Strange. Also, kind of amazing.

"So there's nowhere else to go?" Bear asked.

"Nope!" Mikey said. "Everywhere else is just sky. So instead of exploring out, we explore up, and down, and sideways. We build the whole world ourselves."

Papa knelt beside Bear. "It's okay to feel wobbly about something new," he said. "But we've got each other, and a friend who already knows the rules."

Bear looked at Mikey, already digging his fingers into the dirt. Then at JJ, pulling a small notebook from his hoodie and sketching little boxes for what to build first.

"One tree. One island," Bear said slowly. "We use every bit of it."

"Every bit," Mikey agreed. "That's the fun part."

The nervous flutter in Bear's chest settled, replaced by a small, curious spark. He crouched and pressed his palm flat against the grass. Solid. Real. Theirs.

"Okay," Bear said, standing back up. "Let's build something."

JJ closed his notebook and managed a small smile. "Together?"

"Together," said Papa.

Four builders turned to face their one small, floating chunk of world — ready to make it into something bigger than it looked.

Chapter 2 (491 words)

The first thing they did was punch the tree — all four of them, taking turns, until a little pile of wood logs sat waiting on the grass.

JJ turned the logs into planks, then a crafting table, then careful little stacks of tools. "We need a system," he said, laying everything out in neat rows. "One pickaxe. One axe. Extra planks in case something breaks. If we run out of wood, we're stuck — there's no forest to walk to."

Mikey, meanwhile, had already scooped up a bucket of dirt and was patting it into place along the island's edge. "I'm making us more room!" he announced. "Every scoop of dirt is more island. More island means less falling."

"That's actually smart," JJ admitted, sounding a little surprised.

Papa knelt by the water bucket, using it carefully to start a tiny farm bed, while Bear watched the whole island take shape, block by block. It didn't look like much yet — a stump, a crafting table, a lumpy dirt ledge — but it was theirs, built from nothing but what they'd been given.

"Careful with the dirt," JJ called to Mikey. "We don't have unlimited blocks. If you use it all now, we won't have any left for later."

"I've got plenty!" Mikey said, tossing another scoop down. But a few minutes later, reaching for more, his hand found the bottom of an empty bucket. "...Oh."

Bear looked around. The island was bigger now, but their supplies were smaller. The wood pile was down to a few logs. The dirt bucket was empty. And the little patch of grass under their feet suddenly felt very precious.

"We used a lot, fast," Bear said slowly.

JJ nodded, pulling out his notebook again. "That's why I've been keeping some back," he said, opening a small chest tucked by the crafting table. Inside: a few careful logs, some spare cobblestone from the one boulder they'd found, and a handful of torches. "Not much. But enough that if something goes wrong tonight, we're not starting from zero."

Mikey peeked into the chest and grinned. "You've been squirreling stuff away this whole time?"

"Somebody has to," JJ said, but he didn't sound annoyed. He sounded proud.

Bear crouched next to the little stockpile chest and studied it. One tree. One chunk. And already, some of it was gone forever — turned into tools, dirt, a home. It made him think differently about every single block. Nothing here could be wasted, but nothing had to be, either, if you planned ahead.

"We should keep saving," Bear said. "Just in case."

"Just in case of what?" Mikey asked, glancing up at the sky, which had started, ever so slightly, to dim toward evening.

Nobody answered right away. But Papa reached for the torches in JJ's little chest, and lit one, and planted it at the island's edge, where its warm light pushed back against the coming dark.

Chapter 3 (695 words)

Night came fast on the little island, the way it always did in this blocky world — the sky darkening in stripes, stars popping into place one by one. And with the dark came the sound none of them wanted to hear: a low, groaning shuffle, coming from somewhere near the edge of their tiny patch of grass.

"Zombie," JJ said quietly, already moving. "Two of them. Maybe three."

Bear's heart thumped. On an island this small, there was nowhere to run. No forest to duck into, no hills to hide behind. Just their little base, the crafting table, the dirt ledge Mikey had built — and the dark, pressing in from every side at once.

"Okay," Papa said, keeping his voice steady, the way he always did. "Let's breathe first. One big breath. We know this island. We know each other. We can figure this out."

Bear breathed in, slow, and let it out. The panic in his chest loosened, just enough to think.

JJ was already moving toward the little chest — the one he'd been quietly filling all evening. He pulled out the spare torches, the extra cobblestone, and a short length of fence.

"This is why I kept some back," JJ said, and for the first time all night, he didn't sound worried at all. He sounded ready. "We wall the edges. Torches every few blocks so nothing new spawns. If they're already here, we give them one way in — and we hold it."

"I like this plan," Mikey said, grabbing an armful of cobblestone. "Where do you want it?"

JJ pointed to the narrow gap between the dirt ledge and the crafting table — the only real approach to their little base. "Here. If we block everything else and light up every dark corner, this is the only spot they can reach us from."

They moved together, fast. Papa and Bear stacked cobblestone shoulder-height along the open edges while Mikey planted torches in a tight ring, his turtle hands quick and sure despite how nervous he'd suddenly gone now that the shuffling was close. JJ crouched at the narrow gap with the fence pieces, clicking them into place one after another, checking each connection twice.

A zombie lurched out of the dark and slammed into the fence. It held. Behind it, Bear could see another shape shambling closer, groaning low.

"Torch!" JJ called, and Bear grabbed one from the little pile and passed it over without hesitation. JJ planted it right beside the fence gap, and the light spilled out, pale and warm, pushing the dark back another few feet.

"It's working," Bear said, half in disbelief.

"Of course it's working," Mikey said, grinning even though his voice still shook a little. "We planned! We've got torches and walls and a whole plan for exactly this!"

The second zombie reached the fence and pawed at it uselessly. Papa placed the last cobblestone block, sealing the gap they hadn't finished, and the wall closed all the way around, unbroken. Outside their little ring of torchlight, the zombies groaned and wandered, unable to find a way in.

For a long moment, nobody spoke. They just stood there, the four of them, in their small circle of light, listening to the useless shuffling outside the wall.

"We're okay," Bear said slowly, like he was checking the words were true. "We're actually okay."

"We're better than okay," JJ said, and this time his smile was wide and real. "We're safe because we got ready before we needed to be."

Mikey threw an arm around Bear's shoulders. "See? Told you Skyblock was fun. A little scary. Mostly fun."

Papa knelt and pulled Bear into a hug, feeling his own heartbeat finally slow back down to normal. "You did great," Papa murmured. "All of you. That's what teamwork looks like — everybody bringing something different, and it all fitting together right when it mattered."

Above the torchlight, the stars kept shining, steady and quiet, and beyond the wall, the zombies eventually gave up and wandered off into the endless dark, leaving the little island — and the four friends inside it — safe until morning.

Chapter 4 (529 words)

Morning came soft and gold, and the little island looked almost peaceful, like the night before had been a dream. The wall held strong. The torches still glowed faintly in the daylight. Everyone stretched, yawned, and started packing up the last of their tools.

"We should get the fence panels back," JJ said, already moving toward the gap they'd sealed the night before. "We might need them again, and we shouldn't waste good materials."

But when he reached for the first fence post, it wobbled strangely and tipped — and the little chest sitting behind it, the one holding their last spare torches and the precious remaining cobblestone, slid sideways on the uneven dirt.

"Chest!" Mikey shouted, lunging for it.

Too slow. The chest teetered right on the edge of the island and dropped over the side, spinning down into the endless blue below.

For a second, nobody moved. That chest held almost everything they had left.

"It's gone," JJ said, his voice tight. "We used most of our torches last night. If we run into trouble again before we build more —"

"Wait," Bear said suddenly.

Everyone turned. Bear was staring at the water bucket sitting empty near Papa's little farm bed — the same one they'd used to plant crops. An idea was forming, quick and bright.

"We can't get the chest back," Bear said. "But we don't need everything that was in it. We need light, and we need something solid under our feet if the edge gets shaky again."

He pointed at the dirt ledge Mikey had built the day before — patchy in places, thinner near the corners. "Mikey used dirt to make more island. We can use dirt the same way to patch where the fence tipped. And for light —" Bear looked at the torch still burning steadily by the crafting table — "we don't need a hundred torches. We need one good one, somewhere everyone can always see it."

Papa's eyebrows lifted. "Like a lighthouse."

"Exactly," Bear said, feeling more sure of himself with every word. "One torch, up high, where it can't fall and we can't lose it. Everything else, we build fresh, a little at a time, like yesterday."

JJ looked at the tipped fence post, then at Bear, and slowly, his worried expression eased into something like admiration. "That's... actually a really good plan. Better than just replacing what fell."

Mikey was already stacking dirt blocks to shore up the wobbly corner, humming to himself. "See, JJ? Sometimes you don't need the old plan back. You just need a new one."

Together they packed dirt into the weak spot until it felt solid again, and Papa hoisted a single sturdy torch up onto a little post at the island's highest point, where it could watch over everything below.

Bear stood back and looked at their tiny floating world — patched, glowing, steady. It wasn't exactly the same as it had been an hour ago. It was better, in its own small way, because now they knew they could rebuild whatever they lost.

"We're okay," Bear said again, and this time there wasn't a single wobble in his voice.

Chapter 5 (328 words)

By the time the sun hung high over their little floating island, it didn't look small anymore. Not really. It looked full — full of a crafting table, a tiny farm, a patched dirt ledge, and one steady torch keeping watch from its post at the very top.

Mikey flopped down in the grass with a happy sigh. "Best Skyblock island I've ever built," he announced. "And I've built a lot."

"It's because everybody helped," JJ said, tucking his notebook away for good. "You made more room when we needed it. I kept things ready for when we needed them. And Bear —" he smiled at Bear — "you figured out we didn't need everything back exactly the way it was. We just needed to keep going."

Bear smiled, feeling warm all the way through, like the torchlight from the night before. "We planned ahead," he said, "but when the plan didn't work, we made a new one. That's kind of the whole game, isn't it?"

"That's kind of the whole everything," Papa said, ruffling Bear's hair.

The portal shimmered open near the crafting table, sparkling and soft, ready to carry them home. Mikey gave Bear a turtle-sized hug goodbye, and JJ gave him a careful, proud little nod.

"Same time next adventure?" Mikey asked.

"Any time," Bear said.

They stepped through together, Papa's hand steady around Bear's, and the blue Minecraft sky faded into the familiar warm glow of Papa Bear Home. The couch cushions were soft. The lamp in the corner glowed gold. Outside on the patio, Bear knew, Little Red Bear and Little Polar Bear were resting quietly, just like always.

Bear climbed into bed, pulling the blanket up to his chin, thinking about torches and dirt and one small chunk of grass that had turned into a whole little world.

"Goodnight, Papa," Bear murmured.

"Goodnight, Bear," Papa whispered back, turning off the light.

And the house settled into quiet, safe and warm, until morning.
