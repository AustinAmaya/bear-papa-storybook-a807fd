Preamble

High above the clouds, the Machine World hummed with gears and whirring wings. Bear and Papa had just landed on the sky deck, where Commander Aero waited with a crackle of the radio.

"Something glows in the dark down in the old lantern hall," Commander Aero said. "A serious object. It is very quiet, and very bright."

Bear's ears perked up. "Serious how?"

Papa knelt to Bear's eye level. "Let's breathe first. Then we look carefully."

So they gathered their gear and got ready for the mystery.

Three quick seeds. Chapter 1: how do Bear and Commander Aero react when they first see the glow? Chapter 3: what method guides the big climax? Chapter 4: what helps when the final smaller complication shows up?

Chapter 1

Commander Aero's lights flickered, then went still. "Oh," she said. "It is glowing again."

Bear pressed his nose to the window of the sky deck. Down in the lantern hall, a soft green light pulsed, slow and steady, like a heartbeat.

"Surprised," said Bear. He felt a tingle in his tummy. "Did anybody lose something that glows in the dark?"

Commander Aero clicked her tail-light on and off. "That is an excellent question. Let's check."

Papa crouched beside Bear, right at his eye level. "Breathe first. In for four. Out for four." They breathed together, one, two, three, four. Bear's shoulders came down.

"Okay," Bear said. "Let's look at the list. Things that glow. A nightlight. A star sticker. A glow stick from the party."

Commander Aero hummed. "Please make sure to check what belongs to who."

Bear went through his pockets. He checked his backpack. He checked his jacket sleeves. Then he held up his wrist and stopped.

"Oh!" Bear said. "My watch is missing!"

It was his glow-in-the-dark watch, the one with the big round face and the green numbers. He had taken it off before the trip to the Machine World, and he had put it down somewhere on the deck.

Papa smiled. "Is that the glow we are seeing?"

Bear squinted at the lantern hall. The green light pulsed again, slow and steady. "It might be. But it does not look like a watch."

Commander Aero tilted her head. "Then let's go and see what it is, together."

They took the lift down, one careful step after another. The lantern hall was quiet, with rows of old lamps and dusty crates. In the middle of the floor sat a round object, humming softly, its green light flickering on and off like it was waiting.

Bear took a deep breath. "Okay," he whispered. "What are you?"

The object gave one soft, glowing pulse.

Bear's eyes went wide. "I know you," he said. "I just can't remember where from."

Chapter 2

Bear turned the object over in his hands, slowly. It was warm, and it hummed like a tiny engine. Along one edge were little round lenses, and on top sat a metal knob that looked like a wind-up key.

"Commander Aero," Bear said, "do you know this?"

Commander Aero leaned closer, her lights blinking. "I have seen a design like this before. But I cannot place it yet."

Papa picked up a loose cable from the floor and held it up to the light. "Here is a clue. This cable goes into the back. Something here is powering it."

Bear looked at the back of the object. There was a tiny slot, and beside it a row of blinking dots. The dots blinked in a pattern. Blink, blink, pause. Blink.

"It is talking," Bear said.

"It is signaling," Commander Aero corrected gently. "There is a difference. Signals are for finding things."

Bear counted the blinks under his breath. "One, two, pause, three. One, two, pause, three."

A soft voice came from the corner of the hall. "Beep."

Bear spun around. A tiny square robot rolled out from behind a crate, bumping gently into the wall, then into the next crate.

"Beep!" the robot said again, cheerful. It was a crewmate from the space station, with a little antenna and a bright blue light on top.

Commander Aero whirled her propellers. "Beep! Come and help us. This is a serious object, and it is very, very glowing."

Beep rolled closer. It tapped the edge of the object with one small square hand, then tapped it again, three times. The dots blinked back, one, two, pause, three.

"Beep beep!" Beep said, and bumped into Bear's knee.

"That's a yes," said Papa. "Beep is saying the pattern matches."

Bear grinned. "So the object is answering. I think it is asking us a question."

Commander Aero clicked her tail-light. "Then we need to find out what it is asking."

Papa knelt down, right at the object's side. "Let's test it, slow and careful. Gentle taps, not big swings. We do not want to startle it."

Bear's heart beat fast, but he took a breath. In for four. Out for four. He was ready.

The object gave a long, low hum, then went quiet.

Bear stared at it. "It is waiting for us," he said.

Chapter 3

Bear was thinking hard. He sat cross-legged on the cool floor of the lantern hall and stared at the glowing object. Its green light pulsed, slow and steady, like it was counting.

"If it is waiting," Bear said slowly, "then it is waiting for a person who knows the pattern."

Commander Aero landed beside him with a soft clank. "Then we need a plan. A good one."

Bear picked up a piece of chalk from his jacket. He drew a long line on the floor, and then a circle at one end. "This is the lantern hall. This is the door. This is where the object sits."

"A map," said Papa, nodding. "Good. Maps help us see the whole problem at once."

Bear tapped the circle. "The object blinks in a pattern. One, two, pause, three. Beep knows it too. So we line up everyone's blinks, the same pattern, and see if it answers back."

Beep rolled over, bumping the chalk line, then drew a tiny squiggle beside it with one square hand.

"Beep beep," Beep said, very proudly.

"That is a checkmark," Commander Aero said. "Beep is saying yes."

Papa smiled. "Let us try it, step by step. First, Commander Aero blinks her tail-light. Then Beep taps. Then Bear counts. Then we wait."

They all took their places. Commander Aero clicked her tail-light. One, two, pause, three. Beep tapped the floor. One, two, pause, three. Bear clapped his hands, counting out loud. "One, two, pause, three!"

The object's light flickered. Then it blinked back, exactly the same.

Bear jumped up. "It heard us!"

"It is answering," Papa said softly. "But it is still shy. It has been alone in the dark for a long time."

Bear nodded. He sat down again, quietly. "Then we should be quiet and gentle too."

They waited. The green light pulsed, slow and steady. Then it changed. It blinked one, two, pause, three, and then it stopped on a long, bright glow.

Commander Aero's voice came out soft. "That is the signal for finding something. It is looking for someone."

"Who?" Bear asked.

Right then, a sharp little whistle echoed down the hall. A round robot came rolling in fast, its screen face flashing red. It was Captain Bolts, from the space station, and his voice boomed through the room.

"Attention! Attention! The rest of the station's lights have gone dim. The oxygen panel is blinking too. We need help before the air gets thin!"

Bear's breath caught. Papa stood up at once and put a hand on Bear's shoulder. "Breathe first, Bear. In for four. Out for four."

Bear breathed. One, two, three, four. His hands stopped shaking.

"Okay," Bear said. "The object is looking for someone, and the station needs help. Those are two different things, but maybe they are one problem."

Commander Aero whirled her propellers. "Then we split up. Beep, you check the station lights. Papa, you and Captain Bolts go to the oxygen panel. Bear and I will follow the glow and see where it leads."

Beep beeped in agreement and rolled off, bumping into everything on the way.

Bear hurried beside Commander Aero, the glowing object floating behind them as if it were coming too. Down the hall they went, past rows of blinking lights, past the observation dome, until they came to a small grate in the floor.

Under the grate, something green and bright was blinking back at them.

Bear knelt, his nose right at the metal bars. "Hello," he whispered. "Are you the one who is looking for someone?"

The light pulsed twice, and Bear felt, deep down, that the answer was yes.

Chapter 4

The grate rattled. Bear pulled gently at one corner, and it lifted with a soft click. Underneath was a narrow shaft, dark except for the green glow far below.

"It is down there," Bear said. "The thing that is looking for someone."

Commander Aero hovered over the hole, her propellers humming. "It is too deep for my lights. And too narrow for my wings."

Bear looked at the shaft. It was just a little too narrow for a grown-up arm. But a small hand might fit.

"Wait," said Bear. "Maybe a robot can reach it. Beep is tiny."

Right then, a soft beep echoed up from the corridor. Beep came rolling back, bumping into the wall, then into the chalk map on the floor.

"Beep beep!" Beep said, and tilted its antenna toward the grate.

"A good idea," said Commander Aero. "But Beep needs a light to see by, and a way to hold on."

Bear pulled a glow stick from his pocket. He bent it until it cracked, and a bright white light filled the shaft. "Okay. Light."

Papa's voice came from down the corridor. "Bear! Commander Aero! Captain Bolts and I have fixed the oxygen panel. The air is getting better."

"That's great!" Bear called back. "But we still need to reach the glow."

Captain Bolts rolled up beside them, his screen face cheerful. "Then we need a rope. Lift it gently, and we will not drop anything in there."

Bear looked around. There was a coil of thin wire near the wall, shiny and bendy. He tugged on it and it held. "Wire! Beep can hook the wire on its arm."

"Good thinking," said Papa, crouching beside him. "Let's check the knot first. A loose knot can slip."

Bear tied the wire to the grate's rail, tugged it twice, and then tied it again, carefully. "There. Now it's tight."

Beep hooked its little arm onto the wire, then rolled to the edge of the shaft. Slowly, carefully, it let itself down, one bump at a time, with the glow stick in its other hand.

Bear held the wire steady. He counted the bumps under his breath. "One. Two. Three."

Halfway down, the wire squeaked. It went loose in Bear's hand, just for a second, and his heart jumped into his throat.

"Breathe," Papa said quietly. "In for four. Out for four."

Bear breathed with him. The wire tightened again. Bear felt the tug, steady and strong, and he held on.

Beep stopped at the bottom and gave a happy little chirp. The green light answered, bright and warm.

"Beep found it!" Commander Aero said, relieved.

Bear pulled the wire back up, hand over hand, while Beep rode up slowly, carrying something small and glowing in its arm.

It was a little green lantern, round and quiet, with a tiny wrench sticker on its side.

"Oh!" said Bear. "It was stuck. It was trying to get out so someone could find it."

Commander Aero bowed her propellers. "We found it together."

Chapter 5

The lantern hall grew calm. The green glow settled into a soft, steady shine, and the hum faded into a quiet song.

Bear sat on a cushion of folded blankets, his watch safely back on his wrist. Papa sat beside him, and Commander Aero perched on a shelf, her lights dimmed to a gentle blue.

"Our special outing," said Bear, yawning. "We found the little lantern, and Beep helped."

"Beep is very brave," said Papa. "And so are you. You counted, you breathed, and you asked your friends to help."

Captain Bolts rolled past with a cheerful beep of his own. "The station's lights are back to normal, and the air is sweet again."

Beep rolled up beside Bear and gave his knee a soft, friendly bump.

"Goodnight, Beep," Bear whispered.

Beep beeped softly in reply.

Papa tucked a blanket around Bear's shoulders. The station hummed around them, quiet and warm. Through the window, the stars shone bright and far away.

Bear looked up at Commander Aero. "Do you think the lantern will glow forever?"

"For as long as it needs to," she said kindly. "And it knows where it belongs now."

Bear's eyes grew heavy. He leaned against Papa's soft fur and breathed slowly. In for four. Out for four.

"Goodnight, lantern," he murmured. "Goodnight, Beep. Goodnight, Commander Aero."

The green glow pulsed once more, slow and gentle, and then it was still.

Goodnight, Bear.

Down in the lantern hall, the little lantern sat on its shelf, glowing softly. It did not blink or hum any more. It just shone, warm and steady, like a nightlight that had finally found its home.
